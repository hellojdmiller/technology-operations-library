# Plan, authorize, execute, and verify a change

> Proposed SOP, version 0.1. Apply only after local adoption with named owners, authority, and supported procedures. Examples in this pack are fictional and unexecuted. This document does not authorize a live action.

## Trigger and scope

Use before adding, modifying, or removing a service, application, integration, access configuration, network component, endpoint baseline, or automation that could affect business service. The outcome is either a documented decision to defer/decline or an authorized change with attributable execution, verification, and residual ownership.

Routine support that makes no change may stay in its diagnostic SOP. A catalog request can reference an existing standard-change model when it matches exactly. A restoration incident may need a change record, but the change record must not replace or reset the incident. Link [ticket intake](../../sops/ticket-intake-and-triage.md), [software fulfillment](../../sops/software-request-fulfillment.md), and [service-level management](../../service-level-management.md).

## People and authority

| Role | Accountable contribution |
|---|---|
| Internal change owner and alternate | Scope, coordination, dependencies, calendar, communications, and unfinished work. An MSP execution ticket does not remove this role. |
| Technical reviewer | Risk assessment, implementation/pilot/recovery design, and recommendation with evidence limits. |
| Change authority | Approval or standing delegation for the exact scope and risk; business/data/security decisions as required. The operator cannot invent an exception. |
| Implementer | Execute the authorized version, preserve evidence, stop at thresholds, report uncertainty. |
| Verifier and business acceptance owner | Check the result and intended business task; decide acceptance within assigned authority. |

A small team can combine roles where the adopted model permits it; record the overlap and arrange the independent decision/review appropriate to the consequence. Use the current [authority record](../../succession/ownership-and-escalation.md). A named alternate must have real delegated authority and supported availability. Silence, seniority, possession of a credential, and an urgent ticket are not substitutes.

## Select the route before scheduling

**Standard:** use only a current preauthorized model with established low risk and proven instructions. Match service/entity, environment, product/version range, permitted settings, target population and maximum batch, prerequisites, dependencies, operator qualifications, approved window, required validation, recovery method, verification, and exclusions. Confirm the model's owner, version, authority, validity, and suspension state. A “minor” label or previous success does not create standard status. Any mismatch or unknown goes to normal review or the justified emergency path.

**Normal:** a planned change without that exact match. Use a short asynchronous record and the designated reviewer/approver when risk is bounded. Convene additional decision owners only where dependencies or consequences warrant it; a meeting is not the control.

**Emergency:** a delay would materially worsen an active incident or threat. Record the consequence of waiting, narrow proposed action, emergency authority, available checks, evidence limitations, and recovery decision. Accelerate the process rather than infer approval. Already authorized protective actions can proceed under their standing incident authority while documentation and escalation continue. If authority is missing, reach its alternate; a tool cannot supply it. A missed planning deadline is not automatically an emergency.

ServiceNow describes standard changes as preapproved, low-risk work with a history of success. Its catalog can populate approved template details, but those fields still need to match the actual request. [Standard change catalog](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/it-service-management/change-management/c_StandardChangeCatalogPlugin.html). Atlassian describes emergency assessment/authorization as fast-tracked and recognizes that testing may be reduced under time constraints. [Change definitions](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-service-management-cloud/docs/what-are-changes/).

## Operational steps

1. **Create one scoped record.** Give it a stable ID and link the initiating request/incident. Capture reason, intended outcome, exact targets and exclusions, service/data owners, affected population, dependencies, privileged identities, and the proposed artifact/configuration version. Keep secrets and detailed architecture in controlled references. Name unknowns; do not mark scope confirmed while a material dependency is unresolved.
2. **Assess risk and competing work.** Describe failure modes across confidentiality, integrity, availability, data loss, vendor dependency, and recoverability. Consider who could lose access to administer the recovery. Identify simultaneous changes, provider maintenance, reporting/deal deadlines, travel/coverage constraints, and affected entities. Keep technical risk separate from P1–P4 incident priority. Decide whether the change can be reduced into independent smaller steps without hiding combined risk.
3. **Set a realistic maintenance decision.** Record start/end with time zone, business acceptance of disruption, support/onsite coverage, communications, and a latest safe recovery decision point. Reserve time for verification and recovery inside the approved window. Check current conflicts rather than rely on an old calendar snapshot. ServiceNow exposes conflict checks for blackout, maintenance, and overlapping changes; a check that has not run is not a clean result. [Change request fields](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/it-service-management/change-management/t_CreateAChange.html).
4. **Define the pilot and acceptance evidence.** Choose a representative, bounded population or isolated environment, with explicit success and denied-access checks, observation duration, expected error/latency behavior, and dependencies to exercise. Synthetic data is preferred where it can demonstrate the requirement. A standard model may reuse its current validation evidence only when that model explicitly permits it and the conditions still match. A new version or scope may invalidate that evidence. Google SRE explains that a useful canary needs representative exposure and meaningful observations; a handful of successful actions may miss important failure modes. [Canary evaluation](https://sre.google/workbook/canarying-releases/).
5. **Design recovery before approval.** State which changes can be reversed, what cannot, the last safe reversal point, required prior artifacts/data, who can execute recovery, and its validation. A backup reference alone is not a demonstrated restore path. Distinguish rollback to a known prior state from forward recovery that repairs the new state. For schema, data, external messages, or provider-managed changes, rollback may be impossible or itself unsafe. If only forward recovery is viable, make the additional action, authority, timing, data-loss limit, and fallback explicit. If neither path is credible, defer or obtain the correctly authorized risk/continuity decision; do not write “rollback available.” Microsoft describes both recovery approaches and warns that stateful rollback can be complex. [Safe deployment practices](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/operational-excellence/safe-deployments).
6. **Obtain and preserve the decision.** Present scope/version, impact, evidence, unresolved risk, window, recovery/verification plans, and recommended disposition. Record approved, declined, or deferred with authority and conditions; for standard work cite the current standing approval and exact-match check. Separate spending, data use, and implementation decisions where needed. A scope, artifact, dependency, or timing change returns the affected decision to review. Record a verbal emergency decision promptly with who, when, scope, and subsequent confirmation; do not backdate or invent it.
7. **Run the preflight at the actual start.** Confirm targets and state still match, authority/window remains valid, operators and alternate access are available, relevant evidence is readable, monitoring works, and no conflicting jobs are in flight. Verify the artifact/source/version and recoverable baseline. Review [offline readiness results](../../../n8n/change-readiness-review/README.md) as metadata findings only. Stop if a required fact is false or unknown. An emergency with reduced testing requires the explicit emergency risk decision; an empty pilot field cannot be relabeled successful.
8. **Execute in bounded stages.** Start with the approved pilot/batch and compare observations with its criteria before expanding. Keep one execution coordinator across internal and vendor teams. Record attempts, change IDs, timestamps, target state, and returned operation references. A timeout can occur after a change succeeded; inspect authoritative state before any retry. A correlation ID alone does not prevent duplicate side effects. Use [request pipeline recovery](../../sops/request-pipeline-recovery.md) for reconciliation.
9. **Stop, stabilize, and choose recovery when necessary.** Halt further rollout for unexpected target/scope, loss of management or monitoring, failed acceptance/security checks, material user impact, conflicting state, or reaching the recovery decision point. Preserve evidence and notify the internal owner. Do not improvise additional changes to keep the maintenance promise. Invoke the approved rollback/forward-recovery route, or seek the required new decision. Suspected compromise goes to [security triage](../../sops/security-alert-triage.md); inability to reach an owner uses [ticket escalation](../../sops/ticket-escalation.md).
10. **Verify independently of submission.** Re-read current configuration and action history, check the original business task and important dependencies, validate intended and excluded permissions, and observe the agreed period. A deployment status, provider completion message, or absence of alarms is insufficient alone. Record tested population, results, limitations, and business acceptance. If only part succeeded, identify that population and keep the remainder unresolved. Verify recovery as rigorously as the original change.
11. **Close the correct outcome.** Record implemented and verified, backed out and verified, forward-recovered and verified, cancelled, or incomplete. Incomplete work stays open or moves through an explicitly accepted linked transfer, preserving its unresolved status. Link the incident's separate restoration/resolution/closure events and retain clock history. Update configuration, support instructions, temporary-access cleanup, and follow-up owners before administrative closure. The record of an attempt is not proof of a completed service outcome.

## Review the outcome and keep standard models current

Review emergencies, failed/partially successful changes, significant unexpected effects, and repeated model use failures. Assign the review owner and date at planning or as soon as emergency stabilization permits; use an adopted local target, not an invented universal deadline. Compare intended versus actual scope, detection, decision timing, recovery, user effect, and evidence quality. Give corrections owners and observable checks; a retrospective cannot retroactively authorize the action.

For standard models, set an explicit recertification owner/date and event triggers: platform/version or dependency changes, new permissions or population, failed execution, monitoring gaps, changed authority, or an expired recovery test. Suspend a model when its assumptions no longer hold. Preserve its version and prior records; validate and reauthorize the revised model before reuse. Successful repetitions are inputs, not automatic recertification. Apply [document control](../../document-control.md) and [operating cadence](../../operating-cadence.md).
