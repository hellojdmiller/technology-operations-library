# Start here when the technology owner is unavailable

**Status:** Proposed, vendor-neutral operating guide. The examples are fictional. No contact route, appointment, access path, or recovery capability has been tested by this document.

Use this guide to establish who is coordinating, find the current operating facts, and keep essential work moving within existing authority. It is not a technical recovery procedure or permission to take over another person's accounts.

## Choose the situation before acting

| Situation | Use this path |
|---|---|
| Unexpected absence, with no usable handover | Follow this guide and establish a temporary operating arrangement. |
| A scheduled transfer with available participants | Use [planned handover](planned-handover.md). Do not replace an agreed transfer with an emergency process merely because a document is incomplete. |
| A planned transfer becomes an unexpected absence | Use this guide for immediate continuity, then record changes to the transfer plan. Existing delivery records and decisions retain their own meaning. |
| Suspected compromise, fraud, destructive activity, or a critical outage | Activate the applicable incident or continuity path immediately. The absence is a dependency to manage within that response. |
| Immediate danger to people | Follow the relevant emergency response procedure first. |

Do not delay already authorized protective action while assembling the succession paperwork. Apply the approved incident procedure and preserve evidence as circumstances permit. This guide creates no new containment authority and does not require a responder to wait for a missing handover signature.

## Open the current operating record

Locate the protected **continuity control record** described in [ownership and escalation](ownership-and-escalation.md#keep-changing-facts-in-one-controlled-record). Confirm its revision, owner, last verification, and audience. It should resolve the current role holders, authority, support routes, service dependencies, deadlines, and evidence locations.

If the ordinary document service is unavailable, use the approved alternate access method. Check the alternate copy's revision and capture any uncertainty. A printed copy or exported file needs controlled distribution and replacement; it must not become a second independently edited record.

If no usable record exists, start a protected incident or continuity log. Record each missing fact, the person responsible for confirming it, and the safe interim approach. Do not invent a support number from an email naming pattern or use remembered authority as if it were current.

## Establish coordination and decision rights

The sponsor or authorized alternate confirms the receiving operational owner, coverage period, permitted work, escalation route, and decisions reserved to other roles. Record the actual decision and its basis. An appointment request is not an appointment, and a provider ticket is not approval of its proposed work.

Confirm that the receiving owner can reach the support queue and restricted procedures they are expected to use. Where a provider must recognize authorized requesters, obtain provider-side confirmation through its established verification process. A contact acknowledging a message may still lack the authority to accept a privileged request.

Pause discretionary cleanup while important dependencies are unresolved. Continue authorized routine work and incident response. Any broader change restriction needs an owner, scope, review trigger, and an approved exception path; an open-ended freeze can create its own risk.

## Build a small picture of what needs attention

Ask the following questions in parallel where people and authority allow. Prioritize using business impact and actual dependencies, not a fixed order of products.

| Question | Useful observation | If unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Can responders coordinate? | A successful round trip on the approved channel with a named recipient | Use the documented alternate; record the failed route. |
| What work has a near-term business deadline? | Business owner confirms the capability needed and the consequence of delay | Escalate the priority decision; do not invent a recovery target. |
| Is there an active incident or failed critical job? | Current queue, monitoring, and owner observations, with their coverage limits | Route suspected incidents immediately; keep unobserved services visible. |
| Can ordinary users perform essential work? | A bounded check using an authorized user and a harmless task | Separate a user problem from a shared dependency failure. |
| Can an authorized alternate administer the required scope? | Relevant access and capability check, with logs where available | Use [emergency access readiness](emergency-access-readiness.md); do not borrow the absent person's identity. |
| Who is watching incoming requests and alerts? | Named recipient confirms receipt and the next action | Assign coverage or escalate the gap rather than assuming a shared inbox is watched. |
| Are important recurring obligations covered? | Current operating calendar reconciled to the responsible owner | Record due work and decision authority; do not renew or cancel by inference. |

Use provider status information as one input. A green status page does not establish that the firm's configuration, records, or user path is healthy. Likewise, a failed check does not establish its cause.

## Protect continuity without preserving inappropriate access

Keep business records, account access, and delegated authority as separate decisions. A need to retain a mailbox or audit history does not require leaving interactive access active. Follow the authorized personnel, incident, and records process for the actual situation.

Before a planned identity change, identify dependent integrations, support authorization, recovery contacts, scheduled work, billing notifications, and data ownership. If a security event requires an earlier access restriction, use the incident authority and record the resulting service risks. Do not delay necessary containment simply because dependency mapping is incomplete.

Do not make speculative credential rotations, mass deletions, device wipes, service cancellations, or broad access-policy changes as housekeeping. A required change needs the applicable authority, an impact assessment, verification, and a recovery plan appropriate to that action.

## Leave an operating record another person can use

| Time and zone | Observation or action | Actor and authority reference | Expected and actual result | Evidence or reported basis | Next owner and checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Actual timestamp] | [What was seen, requested, or done] | [Protected references] | [Include pending or failed] | [Internal reference and limitation] | [Role and agreed review] |

Record unsuccessful contact attempts, rejected requests, and unknown coverage. An operator's statement is evidence of what they report; corroborating logs or an independent check can support a stronger conclusion. Preserve that distinction. Correct earlier entries with a dated update rather than silently changing the history.

Use the [decision and action log](../templates/decision-and-action-log.csv) with its [field dictionary](../templates/README.md), or the equivalent records in the existing protected operating system. Keep recommendation, approval, execution, and verification as distinct events. The table above is a readable view of those records, not a requirement for a second parallel log.

The next operating update should state the affected capabilities, current coordinator, verified coverage, unresolved dependencies, decisions needed, interim safeguards, and next review. Share only with the approved audience. Legal or Compliance determines applicable notification and retention requirements; this guide sets no universal deadline or rule of privilege.

## Exit the temporary arrangement deliberately

The sponsor records whether to resume the planned transfer, continue a bounded interim arrangement, activate a fuller continuity plan, or resource additional support. Name the receiving owner for every open item. A quiet queue is not proof of readiness, and an unresolved item does not silently extend the absent or departing person's availability or duties.

Use the [business continuity plan](../../documentation/continuity/business-continuity-plan.md), [incident record](../../documentation/cyber-risk/incident-response-record.md), and [readiness exercise](readiness-exercise.md) for the next stage. Completed records and sensitive evidence belong in the protected operating system, not this repository.
