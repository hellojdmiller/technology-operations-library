# 1. If it points to a person, treat it as theirs

Spot personal information in a spreadsheet, keep the minimum, and know where it may live.

Suggested time: 8–10 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** A marked-up D1 workbook: which columns are personal, which the mailing needs, and where file and extract may live.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

Personal information is wider than a name. It is anything about an identifiable person, directly or by combining details: email addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth, passport or tax numbers, account numbers, financial details, and notes about health or family. Partial identifiers count too. [S2](sources.md#s2), [S3](sources.md#s3)

A list weighs more than one record: losing 214 rows harms 214 people at once, and aggregated stores attract attackers for that reason. So I keep the minimum. The FTC is blunt: if you do not need it, do not keep it or collect it. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S3](sources.md#s3), [S4](sources.md#s4)

Personal details reached from devices or services outside the firm's control carry more risk. That is why D3 names approved locations and rules out personal email, personal cloud, messaging apps, and AI assistants without a written agreement covering the data. [S3](sources.md#s3), [S1](sources.md#s1)

Two things I never handle alone: someone asking to see or delete their data, and a suspected exposure. Both go to the named privacy lead the same day, because rights and deadlines depend on where the person lives. This module is not legal advice. [S5](sources.md#s5), [S1](sources.md#s1)

## Worked example: One column, three questions

**Before**

Passport number is already in the workbook, and Priya wants the whole file, so it goes too.

**After**

Passport number: personal, a strong identifier (P). Not needed for a mailing (N). It stays in the workbook on the managed drive; the Q3 extract holds preferred name and work email only.

**Why:** Three questions settle most columns: does it point to a person, does the task need it, where may it live? Asked column by column, they turn worry into a specific extract in a specific place.

## Check one decision

Priya's D2 request: the whole workbook to a personal address, tonight. What is the supported response?

- **A.** Send it; she has edit access and promised to delete it.
- **B.** Build a two-column extract in the approved folder, share it to her work account, and say why.
- **C.** Refuse, and report Priya to Dana as an exposure.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **B**.

**A:** Edit access is not permission to move data out of the approved location. D3 rules out personal email, a promise to delete cannot be checked, and 275 people's identifiers would travel for a two-column task.

**B:** This meets D2's real need within D3's rules: minimum columns, an approved location, access through her firm account from home. Faster than arguing about the whole file, too.

**C:** Nothing has left the approved location, so there is no exposure to report; D3 reserves that for actual or suspected leaks. Refusing without an alternative leaves the mailing undone.

</details>
