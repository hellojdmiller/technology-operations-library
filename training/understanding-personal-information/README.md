# Personal Information in Plain Words

One short lesson on recognizing personal information in ordinary files, keeping only what a task needs, and knowing where it may live and who to tell.

I handle other people's details every week: an investor's mobile number, a candidate's home address, a colleague's bank account for payroll. None of it is mine. This short module is about noticing when a piece of information points to a person, and treating it with that in mind.

The rule I use is simple. If someone could be picked out from the information, alone or combined with something else we hold, it is personal information. Names, email addresses, identifiers, financial details, and notes about health or family all count. So does a list of any of these.

Obligations differ by where the people live, and I am not offering legal advice here. Your firm's privacy lead and adviser own that question. What I can offer is the everyday habit: collect the minimum, keep it in approved places, and pass requests and suspected exposures to the named person quickly.

**Format:** Short module · **Track:** Security awareness · **For:** Everyone who handles names, contact details, identifiers, or financial details of people: clients, investors, employees, or candidates.
