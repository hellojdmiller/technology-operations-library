# What a Safeguards Program Actually Contains

One short lesson on the nine components a written information security program is generally expected to contain, and how a small firm shows each with dated evidence rather than a binder.

Sooner or later someone asks a small investment firm to produce its information security program. It might be a prospective investor's questionnaire, an auditor, an insurer, or a regulator's examiner. The firm usually has more than it thinks and less than it needs, and nobody has written down which is which.

This module names the nine components such a program is generally expected to contain and shows how a firm of about fifteen people covers each with dated evidence. I use the SEC's amended Regulation S-P and the FTC Safeguards Rule as examples of what reviewers expect, not as a statement of what applies to you.

Nothing here is legal advice. Which rules bind a firm depends on its registration, its clients, and where it operates, and that is a question for counsel. The exercise takes 12 to 15 minutes, needs no tool account, and ends with a gap list you could hand to a partner.

**Format:** Short module · **Track:** Technology operations · **For:** Partners, operations and compliance leads, and the technology lead at a small investment firm (venture capital or private equity) who will be asked to show how client and investor information is protected.
