# 1. Nine things a reviewer will ask to see

Turn 'we have a policy' into nine rows of dated evidence, gaps, and owners.

Suggested time: 12–15 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** A nine-row gap list for the packet's firm: evidence that exists, evidence missing, and an owner for each component.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

A reviewer asking for the safeguards program is asking about nine things: a named owner, a written risk assessment, access controls, encryption of sensitive information, vendor oversight, an incident response program with notification procedures, training, monitoring and testing, and periodic review. The FTC's Safeguards Rule guide lists nearly the same set. [S2](sources.md#s2)

Which rule binds you depends on registration and jurisdiction; ask counsel. As examples, the SEC's 2024 Regulation S-P amendments require registered advisers to keep a written incident response program, oversee service providers, notify affected individuals within 30 days, and record compliance. The FTC rule reaches firms outside SEC registration. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S2](sources.md#s2)

A reviewer wants proof each thing happens, not a binder: a dated assessment, an MFA export, a vendor list with review dates, an incident plan with a notification clock, a training record. NIST's small business guide says the same: name who is responsible, inventory assets, require MFA, keep a plan listing who to call. [S3](sources.md#s3)

## Worked example: From a policy sentence to dated evidence

**Before**

Access controls: our security policy requires strong passwords and MFA for all staff. Policy attached.

**After**

Access controls. Exists: MFA enforced on 14 accounts, console export dated 30 August 2026. Missing: quarterly access review, leaver checklist. Owner: Jordan Bell, technology lead; first review due 31 October 2026.

**Why:** Every claim is dated, the gaps are stated, and one named person owns them by a date.

**Deliverable:** Nine rows, each written as exists; missing; owner.

## Try a changed case

Suppose there is no technology lead and Brackenmoor handles all IT. What changes in your list?

<details>
<summary>Changed-case answer</summary>

The owner column, not the evidence column. Each technical row still needs an owner inside the firm, probably the operations lead or CFO, who asks Brackenmoor for evidence and files it. A provider can produce an MFA export; it cannot own the program. It also becomes a vendor row.

## Check one decision

D2 question 7.6 asks for the incident response plan and notification period. What is the honest position today?

- **A.** Attach the 2021 policy; it mentions reporting incidents.
- **B.** Record 'none', name the CFO and CCO as owner, draft the plan before 16 October.
- **C.** Say Brackenmoor handles incidents, so the plan is theirs.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **B**.

**A:** D1 says no procedure exists; the policy only tells staff to tell a partner. Calling it the plan overstates, and the reviewer will ask for roles and a timeline.

**B:** Supported. It records the true position, gives the row a D3 owner, and makes the questionnaire deadline the plan's deadline.

**C:** Brackenmoor is one of D1's 23 vendors, not the program's owner. The investor asked the firm, and notifying is the firm's decision; the provider belongs inside the firm's plan.

</details>
