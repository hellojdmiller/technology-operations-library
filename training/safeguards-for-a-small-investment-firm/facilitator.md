# What a Safeguards Program Actually Contains: facilitator guide

A colleague can run this course as one session of about 27–40 minutes, or as 1 short sessions. No specialist background is required to facilitate; the answer keys are in each lesson.

## Before the session

- Send or print the [fictional packet](source-packet.md) and the [worksheet](worksheet.md).
- Say plainly that everyone and everything in the packet is fictional, and that no real client, investor, employee, or credential data is used or discussed.
- Agree how answers will be shared: spoken, written, or in pairs. Reading and response alternatives are welcome.

## Ground rules

- Try the task before opening the example answer. The example is a comparison, not a mark scheme.
- Explaining a choice matters more than matching the wording.
- A first attempt with honest gaps is more useful than a polished copy of the example.

## Lesson 1: Nine things a reviewer will ask to see (12–15 minutes)

**Outcome:** A nine-row gap list for the packet's firm: evidence that exists, evidence missing, and an owner for each component.

**Run it:** read the explanation together or ask participants to read it, walk through the worked example, then give the task. Allow most of the time for the task and the comparison.

**Discussion prompt:** Which row was hardest to give an owner, and what does that say about where the program stalls?

**Changed case:** Suppose there is no technology lead and Brackenmoor handles all IT. What changes in your list?

<details>
<summary>Changed-case answer</summary>

The owner column, not the evidence column. Each technical row still needs an owner inside the firm, probably the operations lead or CFO, who asks Brackenmoor for evidence and files it. A provider can produce an MFA export; it cannot own the program. It also becomes a vendor row.

**Check:** D2 question 7.6 asks for the incident response plan and notification period. What is the honest position today? The supported choice is **B**.

<details>
<summary>Why the other options fall short</summary>

**A:** D1 says no procedure exists; the policy only tells staff to tell a partner. Calling it the plan overstates, and the reviewer will ask for roles and a timeline.

**C:** Brackenmoor is one of D1's 23 vendors, not the program's owner. The investor asked the firm, and notifying is the firm's decision; the provider belongs inside the firm's plan.

</details>
