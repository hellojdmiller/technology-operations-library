# 1. Draft with it, decide without it

Sort each proposed use by whether the assistant drafts words you own or shapes a decision about a person.

Suggested time: 9–12 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** A sorted list of D1's uses under draft with help, decide without it, and ask first, with a reason for each.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

An assistant earns its place in people work on words you own: a first draft of a job description, a summary of the panel's notes, a tone check. One rule sits underneath: a human who can explain the reasons makes and owns the decision.

It is not the decision-maker. Screening, ranking, scoring, and inferring characteristics from text or video are decisions about people. A regulator's audit found recruitment tools inferring gender and ethnicity from names and letting recruiters filter on protected characteristics. [S1](sources.md#s1)

Bias in training data reproduces at scale. A model learns from past decisions, and NIST notes that systemic, statistical, and human-cognitive bias can each occur without intent. One tired manager affects one shortlist; one tilted tool tilts every applicant. [S4](sources.md#s4)

You owe the person affected an explanation; guidance and the OECD principles center transparency and accountability. Could you tell the candidate ranked 47th why? Keep personal data out of unapproved tools. Requirements differ by jurisdiction, and some places regulate solely automated decisions, so a new use or location means asking first. [S2](sources.md#s2), [S3](sources.md#s3), [S5](sources.md#s5)

## Worked example: A good use still needs a human read

**Before**

You will be a digital native at home with modern tools, a native English speaker who writes clear notices.

**After**

You will be comfortable with booking tools, or willing to learn ours, and write clear notices and emails in English.

**Why:** Drafting the description is a fine use. 'Digital native' signals an age and 'native English speaker' an origin; neither is the requirement, so the revision names the skill.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **B**.

**A:** The label does not change the effect. An order deciding whom a tired reviewer reaches last shapes the shortlist, which is what D2 turns on.

**B:** Counting criteria against the firm's own description is close to drafting, but ordering applicants by it edges toward screening, and D2 says ask before a new use starts.

**C:** D2 does not ban every use; it allows drafting and summarizing a person's own material in approved tools. Overstating the rule makes the next proposal harder to sort.
