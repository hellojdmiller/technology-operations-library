# Research behind AI in People Decisions

Research reviewed: 2026-09-21. The lessons, fictional packet, hints, answer keys, and timing are original proposals. No learner trial has been performed for this course.

## S1

**[AI tools used in recruitment: audit outcomes report](https://ico.org.uk/action-weve-taken/audits-and-overview-reports/2024/11/ai-tools-used-in-recruitment/)**

Publisher: UK Information Commissioner's Office

Published or updated: 2024-11-06

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** The landing page and the linked November 2024 report summarize consensual audits of AI recruitment tool providers. The report found some tools let recruiters filter out candidates with certain protected characteristics, and others estimated or inferred gender, ethnicity, and other characteristics from applications or names rather than asking; some collected far more personal information than needed. The ICO made almost 300 recommendations, all accepted, covering fairness, transparency, data minimization, DPIAs, and human review.

**Our application:** Supports the lesson's line between drafting and deciding, the point that inferring characteristics from text is a decision about a person, and the note that filtering and ranking features carry discrimination risk.

**Limits:** UK data protection findings about tool providers, not a study of how often such tools discriminate in practice, and not a statement of the law in any other jurisdiction. The lesson gives no legal advice.

**Revisit when:** When the ICO publishes further recruitment guidance or its 'Recruitment rewired' work updates these findings.

## S2

**[Guidance on AI and data protection](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/guidance-on-ai-and-data-protection/)**

Publisher: UK Information Commissioner's Office

Published or updated: 2023-03-15 (last updated, as stated on the page)

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Sets out how data protection principles apply to AI, with sections on fairness, algorithmic fairness, bias and discrimination, sources of bias across the AI lifecycle, and how solely automated decision-making and its safeguards relate to fairness under UK GDPR Article 22; it points to the ICO's separate 'Explaining decisions made with AI' guidance for transparency and explainability.

**Our application:** Supports the paragraph on owing the affected person an explanation, and the point that bias can enter at problem formulation and data collection, not only in the model.

**Limits:** UK-specific regulatory guidance framed around UK GDPR. It does not describe the law elsewhere, and this module is not legal advice. Explainability detail sits in a companion ICO product, not on this page.

**Revisit when:** When the ICO updates the guidance, or when the organization's operating jurisdictions change.

## S3

**[Automated decision-making, including profiling](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/automated-decision-making-and-profiling/)**

Publisher: UK Information Commissioner's Office

Published or updated: 2026-03-31 (latest update, as stated on the page)

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Describes automated decision-making as decisions based solely on automated processing, with no meaningful human involvement, that have a legal or similarly significant effect on a person, and sets out the safeguards including what counts as human intervention.

**Our application:** Used only for the general point that some jurisdictions regulate solely automated decisions with significant effects, and that a token human glance is not meaningful involvement.

**Limits:** UK law as interpreted by the UK regulator, recently updated; the module does not restate its tests or apply them to any real employer, and requirements differ elsewhere.

**Revisit when:** When the ICO revises the guidance or the underlying UK legislation changes.

## S4

**[Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0), NIST AI 100-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)**

Publisher: National Institute of Standards and Technology

Published or updated: 2023-01 (month only)

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** A voluntary framework whose trustworthiness characteristics include valid and reliable, accountable and transparent, explainable and interpretable, and fair with harmful bias managed. It names three categories of AI bias, systemic, computational and statistical, and human-cognitive, each of which can occur without prejudice or intent, and notes that human roles in overseeing AI decisions need to be clearly defined.

**Our application:** Supports the paragraph on why bias reproduces at scale and the framing that accountability presupposes transparency.

**Limits:** Voluntary and sector-agnostic; it is not employment guidance. The US EEOC's 2023 technical assistance on AI and Title VII and the Department of Labor's 2024 AI and worker well-being principles were removed from the agencies' websites in January 2025; at review the EEOC pages returned 404 and the DOL page could not be opened (HTTP 403), so neither is cited here.

**Revisit when:** When NIST revises the framework or US agencies publish replacement employment guidance.

## S5

**[OECD AI Principles](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles)**

Publisher: Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD.AI)

Published or updated: Adopted May 2019, updated May 2024

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Five values-based principles for trustworthy AI: inclusive growth, sustainable development and well-being; human rights and democratic values, including fairness and privacy; transparency and explainability; robustness, security and safety; and accountability.

**Our application:** Supports the duty to explain and the accountability framing in the lesson, as a widely adopted intergovernmental statement rather than a national rule.

**Limits:** High-level principles addressed to governments and AI actors; they do not say how a hiring manager should use an assistant or create any obligation on an employer by themselves.

**Revisit when:** When the OECD next revises the Recommendation.
