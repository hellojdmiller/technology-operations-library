# AI in People Decisions

A short module on using an assistant to draft in people work while keeping screening, ranking, scoring, and the decision itself with a named human who can explain it.

Hiring, reviewing, and promoting are the decisions colleagues remember longest, and the ones they most deserve to have explained. An assistant can help with the writing around those decisions. I do not want it making them, ranking people, or guessing at who someone is from how they write.

This module follows one fictional hiring round. You will sort a manager's proposed uses into three columns: draft with help, decide without it, and ask first. The sorting is the habit; the reason you write beside each item is what you will reach for when a real proposal lands.

Legal requirements differ by jurisdiction, and some places regulate automated employment decisions directly. Nothing here is legal advice. When a use touches personal data or a decision about a person, the answer in this module is to ask your policy owner or counsel before you proceed, not after.

No AI account is needed. Work on paper or in a document, review the fictional packet, and compare with the example. If you try a prompt in an organization-approved tool, use only this packet. The suggested 9–12 minutes is a starting estimate; take longer if you need it.

**Format:** Short module · **Track:** AI at work · **For:** Managers, HR, and anyone who hires, reviews, or promotes. · **Before this course:** AI at Work
