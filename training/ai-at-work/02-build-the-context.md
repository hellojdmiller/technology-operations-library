# 2. Give the right information

Help the writer see what is current, what is background, and what is still unknown.

Suggested time: 15–20 minutes. Work on paper or in a document; an AI account is optional.

**Outcome:** Build a small source-backed context note that resolves the old time and room while preserving genuine open questions.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

I want the writer to see which information answers the task and why it can be used. Keep source labels and dates so you can check details later.

D1 is early planning, D2 is Noor's confirmation, and D3 sets the approval rules. D4 is later in date but still only a draft. 'Use the newest document' would select the wrong source.

D2 changes the time and room while keeping the purpose, numbers, and assignments. Compare actual statements instead of discarding everything older.

Microsoft and Google recommend relevant context and clear expectations. I would apply that guidance with a short fact list, separate open items, and source labels. This exercise does not measure model context capacity or compare prompts. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S2](sources.md#s2)

## Worked example: A later date is not the whole answer

**Before**

The newest item is D4, so use its 10:00 start, Cedar room, and approved refreshments.

**After**

Current logistics: 8 October, 14:00–15:00 Pacific, Maple room, from Noor's confirmation in D2. D1's earlier time and room are replaced. Refreshments remain pending under D2 and D3. D4 is a draft to correct, not a source of confirmation.

**Why:** The source's purpose and authority matter alongside its date. A draft cannot approve its own claims.

## Your turn
