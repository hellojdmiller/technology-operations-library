# 1. Give AI a useful job

Turn a vague request into a short assignment you can check.

Suggested time: 15–20 minutes. Work on paper or in a document; an AI account is optional.

**Outcome:** Write a clear request for a team update and action list, including the audience, sources, open questions, and draft-only boundary.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

I start with the job, not a clever prompt. Who needs the result, and what should they do with it? Colleagues need the workshop arrangements and next actions. Noor needs a draft that makes undecided details visible.

'Make this sound good' leaves important choices open. 'Draft an internal update and an owner/action/deadline list using D1–D3' gives you something specific to review.

You do not need a long formula. Microsoft starts with a clear goal; Google suggests relevant context and format. These are starting points, not accuracy guarantees. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S2](sources.md#s2)

Keep writing separate from action. A draft does not book a room, approve refreshments, or send an invitation. Noor has asked to review it first.

## Worked example: From a vague request to a checkable assignment

**Before**

Make a friendly workshop announcement. Fill in anything missing so it sounds ready.

**After**

Draft a short internal workshop update and a separate action list for Noor to review. Use only D1–D3. Follow Noor's current arrangements, preserve details that are still pending, and use an owner/action/deadline format. Do not invent dates or approvals, send messages, or make bookings.

**Why:** The revised request defines the reader, result, evidence, and boundary. It makes accuracy more important than sounding finished.
