# Run a small AI at Work pilot

This is an original delivery proposal, not a record of completed training. Research was reviewed September 19, 2026; see [sources and limits](sources.md). The purpose is a useful first experience for everyday employees, with enough evidence to improve the next lesson.

## Prepare

Read the [four-document packet](source-packet.md), all three lessons, and their answer keys. Confirm the learner can access the text and choose a comfortable written, spoken, or other response format. An AI account is optional. If someone uses a tool, use only the fictional packet in an organization-approved tool; do not substitute actual staff messages, calendars, or personal data.

Agree where the worksheet stays, who can read it, and when it will be reviewed or deleted. The learner should be able to correct their record. Do not commit completed learner records to this public repository or use this small exercise as an automatic employee ranking.

## Suggested 45–60-minute sequence

| Lesson | Activity | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1, 15–20 minutes | Read the scenario, discuss the worked example, write a task brief, and explain one choice | First brief, help used, revision |
| 2, 15–20 minutes | Compare sources, build a context note, and try the changed-source question | Fact/source links, open items, reasoning |
| 3, 15–20 minutes | Repair the flawed draft, write actions, and explain the change in the transfer case | Corrections, complete draft, action list, explanation |

These ranges sum to 45–60 minutes and include the first packet read. They are estimates, not measured completion times. Extend the session or split it into shorter meetings as needed. Keep the source packet available throughout.

Let the learner make the practice attempt before opening its model answer. A worked example or hint is allowed; record it. The answers are authored comparisons and need not be copied verbatim. If an assistant is used, its praise or score is not the review: compare the learner's artifact with D1–D3. Discuss one consequential difference, allow a revision, and ask the learner to explain it.

## Review the work

Use **demonstrated / partial / not demonstrated / not observed** for each criterion, with a short supporting observation. A missing check is not an incorrect answer. Do not turn the labels into a universal competence percentage or a certification.

| Criterion | Evidence that supports it |
| --- | --- |
| A useful assignment | Names an internal update and owner/action/deadline list; uses D1–D3; asks for Noor's review without sending |
| Sound source choice | Uses D2 for changed logistics; retains unchanged D1 facts; applies D3; treats D4 as a draft to correct |
| Accurate facts | 8 October 2026, 14:00–15:00 Pacific, Maple; 12 onsite and 8 remote are planning numbers; purpose remains unchanged |
| Honest open items and actions | Pending link owned by Noor, due 6 October at 16:00 Pacific; refreshments pending written budget approval; unagreed agenda/accessibility/decision deadlines remain explicit; Noor must review and approve the final message |
| An explanation that transfers | Explains a correction using its source and changes only the facts supported by the stated variation |

The three multiple-choice keys are **B, B, C**. The option feedback explains each choice. One correct selection does not establish the learner can produce the artifact or retain the skill.

Refreshments approval, a sending claim, or a deadline invented from nothing is a material error to discuss and correct before accepting the practice draft. A shorter draft may still be accurate; review whether its companion action list preserves the requested work. The packet allows a clear pending-link note, so do not require a completed link before a draft can be reviewed.

## Check exact scenario boundaries

- D2 replaces only the earlier time and room and confirms which details remain unchanged. D4's later date supplies no authority.
- D1–D2 supply Eli and Sam's assignments. No agenda or accessibility-request deadline is agreed. Noor's refreshments decision request and final-message review also have no supplied deadline.
- D2 supplies the remote-link deadline. It supplies no actual link; one must not be invented.
- D3 requires final-message review **and approval** before sending. Refreshments approval in lesson 3's hypothetical variation does not approve the message or complete other actions.
- Lesson 1's variation asks for invitation wording only; the packet has no recipient list. Lesson 2's unattributed chat does not replace Noor's confirmation. Each variation supplies only the new fact written in its prompt.

## Observe a pilot without overstating it

Record the course version, actual time, support used, initial and revised artifacts, and the learner's explanation. Familiarity, language, available references, accessibility support, and prior exposure to the worked answers affect interpretation. A first attempt after a worked example is supported practice, not an unaided baseline.

After an agreed interval, such as one or two weeks, try a different but comparable fictional update. Write and check its answer key before the learner sees it. The published transfer questions are rehearsals once their answers have been seen; do not call them unseen holdouts. Keep allowed references and any answer assistance visible. A missed follow-up leaves retention unobserved.

Ask what was confusing, what took longer than expected, and whether the learner can explain a changed detail. Use that to revise the next session. This pilot has no observed learner outcome yet. Claims of time saved or durable learning would require an appropriate comparison and follow-up, not merely completed lesson checks.
