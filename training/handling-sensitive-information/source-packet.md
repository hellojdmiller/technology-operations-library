## D3: Data handling standard (excerpt)

Date: 2026-06-01 · Fictional policy: classification levels, approved sharing methods, prohibited channels, and reporting

Classification. Public: published or cleared for publication; no harm if seen by anyone. Internal: everyday working material; mild embarrassment or inconvenience if it leaked, and no individual is harmed. Confidential: information about a company, a deal, or a counterparty that would harm that party or the firm if it leaked.

Restricted: information that would harm a specific person or investor, or give someone control of our systems or money. This always includes personal data such as dates of birth, home addresses, credit history, and identity documents; investor identities, commitments, and terms; and credentials, wire instructions, and one-time codes.

The test. Before you share, ask who is harmed if this leaks, and how badly. Answer with a person, a company, or the firm. If the answer is a person or an investor, the item is restricted regardless of how ordinary the file looks. A list of names is data about those people.

Approved sharing. Internal: the firm's document library. External: a link to named recipients who must sign in, view-only unless editing is the purpose, expiring within 30 days. Share the specific folder or file the recipient needs, never a folder that also holds other parties' material or a higher classification.

Restricted material leaves the firm only with the information owner's written approval. Attachments are copies the firm cannot take back. Use one only when a link fails for the recipient and the item is confidential or below. 'Anyone with the link' is never used for confidential or restricted material.

Prohibited. Personal email accounts, personal cloud storage, and unapproved messaging or AI tools may not receive firm information at internal or above. Entering firm information into any tool is a share to that tool's operator; the 'Using AI Without Leaking' course covers approved tools.

Mistakes. Tell the technology team and the information owner within the hour of noticing a mis-share, before you are sure of the details. Recall is not a fix. Record what went where, when, and what was done.

The technology team can revoke links, remove guest access, pull access and audit records, and advise on any notification duty. Only the sender can tell the recipient what happened and ask them to delete it.
