# 2. Share deliberately

Choose the recipient, the permission, and the end date before the file.

Suggested time: 15–20 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** Write a sharing decision for the request in D1, with every setting spelled out in words.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

A link to named people who sign in is a door I can lock again: I see who holds it, change what they can do, and set the day it stops working. An attachment is a copy that leaves with no lock. Named link first; an attachment only when a link cannot work.

Microsoft describes an 'anyone with the link' link as one that opens without signing in, keeps working when forwarded, and leaves no record of who has access or who opened it. Google's controls are the same idea: specific people, restricted general access, no resharing by editors, an expiry on work accounts. [S3](sources.md#s3), [S5](sources.md#s5)

Personal email and personal cloud storage are out. The FTC warns against sending sensitive personal data by email, and D3 bars personal accounts above public. When someone asks for a whole folder, build one holding only what they need. Typing into an AI tool is a share too; 'Using AI Without Leaking' covers it. [S1](sources.md#s1)

## Worked example: Same request, different answer

**Before**

Hi Oskar, here is the folder link. Anyone with the link can edit and it never expires, so your team can get straight in.

**After**

Hi Oskar, the folder with our agreed request list is shared to you and your counsel's named contact by email: sign-in required, view only, expiring 21 October 2026. Send names as people rotate on and I will add them that day. Questions go in the editable tracker beside it.

**Why:** Oskar gets what diligence needs and a place for questions; the firm keeps control of who opens the files and for how long.

## Check one decision

Oskar wants 'anyone with the link' so colleagues can rotate on without invitations. Which response fits D1 to D3?

- **A.** Agree, but make the link view only so nobody can change the files.
- **B.** Share to named people who sign in, view only, with expiry, adding new colleagues by name.
- **C.** Send the files as email attachments so link settings stop mattering.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **B**.

**A:** View only limits damage, not audience. D3 bars 'anyone' links for confidential material, D2 shows restricted items in this folder, and Microsoft notes a forwarded anyone link works for whoever holds it, unrecorded. [S3]

**B:** This meets Oskar's need and D3's rule: every reader is known, access can be removed per person, and the expiry ends exposure with the deal.

**C:** An attachment drops every control: no expiry, no revocation, no forwarding record. D3 allows one only when a link fails, which Oskar has not said, and D2's restricted items cannot be attached at all.

</details>
