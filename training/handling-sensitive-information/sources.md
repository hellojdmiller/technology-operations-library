# Research behind Sharing Without Leaking

Research reviewed: 2026-09-21. The lessons, fictional packet, hints, answer keys, and timing are original proposals. No learner trial has been performed for this course.

## S1

**[Protecting Personal Information: A Guide for Business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/protecting-personal-information-guide-business)**

Publisher: Federal Trade Commission (United States)

Published or updated: October 2016 (as stated on the page)

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Five principles for businesses: take stock of the personal information you hold, scale down to what you need, lock what you keep, dispose of the rest properly, and plan ahead for incidents. It advises against sending sensitive personal data by email, recommends least-privilege access, and asks for a written response plan.

**Our application:** Lesson 1 uses take stock and scale down to justify reading a diligence folder before sharing it. Lesson 2 uses the email warning to support D3's ban on personal accounts and casual attachments. Lesson 3 borrows plan ahead for the correction note.

**Limits:** US consumer-protection guidance for general small businesses, dated 2016. It does not address investment firms, cloud sharing links, or any specific platform, and it is not a legal analysis of any jurisdiction's duties.

**Revisit when:** Recheck if the FTC revises or replaces the guide, or if the firm's regulatory obligations change.

## S2

**[Personal data breaches: a guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/report-a-breach/personal-data-breach/personal-data-breaches-a-guide/)**

Publisher: Information Commissioner's Office (United Kingdom)

Published or updated: 20 August 2025 (as stated on the page)

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Defines a personal data breach as a security breach leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data; lists sending personal data to the wrong recipient as an example; requires notifiable breaches to be reported to the ICO within 72 hours of awareness; and requires the facts, effects, and remedial action to be documented for every breach, notifiable or not.

**Our application:** Lesson 1 uses the definition to show that a list of names is personal data whose disclosure counts. Lesson 3 uses the wrong-recipient example, the 72-hour clock, and the recording duty to justify reporting in minutes and writing the facts down.

**Limits:** UK GDPR guidance. Whether the 72-hour duty applies depends on the firm's jurisdiction and the data involved; the course borrows the timing and recording habits, not the legal conclusion. It does not cover non-personal deal information such as a negotiating position.

**Revisit when:** Recheck when the ICO updates the guide or when the firm's applicable data protection regime changes.

## S3

**[Manage sharing settings for SharePoint and OneDrive in Microsoft 365](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/turn-external-sharing-on-or-off)**

Publisher: Microsoft Learn

Published or updated: 2026-06-30 (article date)

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Describes the external sharing levels (Anyone, New and existing guests, Existing guests, Only people in your organization). 'Anyone' links open without authenticating, forwarded links work internally or externally, and the organisation cannot track who has access or who has accessed the item. Administrators can require Anyone links to expire within a set number of days and restrict them to view permission. 'Specific people' is described as the most restrictive link option. When sharing is restricted or turned off, guests typically lose access within one hour.

**Our application:** Lesson 2 uses the description of Anyone links to explain why D2's link is a problem and why named recipients, view only, and expiry are the default. Lesson 3 uses the absence of access records for Anyone links to explain what the technology team cannot tell you after an over-share.

**Limits:** Administrator documentation for one platform. Menus differ by tenant configuration and change often, and a firm's defaults may already restrict some options. It says nothing about email or other platforms; Google's controls are covered in S5.

**Revisit when:** Recheck when the article date changes or when the firm changes its sharing platform or tenant settings.

## S4

**[Recall or replace a sent email in Outlook](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/recall-or-replace-a-sent-email-35027f88-d655-4554-b4f8-6c0729a723a0)**

Publisher: Microsoft Support

Published or updated: undated

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Recall works only when both sender and recipient have Microsoft 365 work or school accounts in the same organisation and the recipient has not yet opened the message. It is not available for recipients outside the organisation or for personal accounts, and it cannot retrieve a message that has been read.

**Our application:** Lesson 3 uses these conditions to show that recall does nothing for the external, already-read email in D4, and that it should not be counted as a correction step.

**Limits:** Describes one email product. Other clients have different or no recall features, and none can retrieve a message an external recipient has already read. The page shows no publication date, so its currency has to be judged by rechecking it.

**Revisit when:** Recheck if the firm changes email platform or if Microsoft revises the recall conditions.

## S5

Publisher: Google Drive Help

Published or updated: undated

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Explains how to remove a person's access, change General access from 'Anyone with the link' to 'Restricted' so only people with access can open the file, stop editors from changing permissions and resharing, prevent viewers and commenters from downloading, printing, and copying, and add an expiration date on access for work or school Google accounts. It notes that files in a shared folder inherit the folder's sharing.

**Our application:** Lesson 2 uses these controls to show that the settings the course asks for exist on Google's platform as well as Microsoft's, and that folder-level sharing overrides file-level care.

**Limits:** End-user help for one platform; some options depend on account type and administrator settings. The page is undated, so it may lag the product. It does not address email or classification.

**Revisit when:** Recheck when the firm's platform changes or when Google changes the sharing dialog.
