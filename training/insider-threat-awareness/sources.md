# Research behind Insider Risk Without Suspicion

Research reviewed: 2026-09-21. The lessons, fictional packet, hints, answer keys, and timing are original proposals. No learner trial has been performed for this course.

## S1

**[Defining Insider Threats](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/physical-security/insider-threat-mitigation/defining-insider-threats)**

Publisher: CISA

Published or updated: undated

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Defines an insider as anyone who has or had authorized access to or knowledge of an organization's resources, and insider threat as the potential to use that access, wittingly or unwittingly, to do harm. Lists unintentional threats (negligence, such as losing a device; accidental, such as a misdirected email or an opened phishing attachment), intentional threats, collusive threats, and third-party threats from contractors or vendors.

**Our application:** The opening paragraph and its examples of convenience and mistakes rest on these categories. The contractor line in D2 reflects the third-party category.

**Limits:** Gives categories and examples, not proportions. It does not say that most incidents are unintentional; that framing is my own operating experience, stated as such.

**Revisit when:** If CISA restructures its insider threat pages or changes the definitions.

## S2

**[Insider Threat Mitigation Guide](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/insider-threat-mitigation-guide)**

Publisher: CISA

Published or updated: 2026 edition; the page dates its companion resource 2026-01-28

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Describes a program for understanding insider threats, identifying the forms they take, and implementing mitigation, including 'countering unintentional incidents' alongside preventing loss of intellectual property and compromise of sensitive data. Frames threat management as intervention that balances organizational protection and individual care.

**Our application:** Supports treating unintentional incidents as part of the same program and taking a non-accusatory approach to concerns.

**Limits:** The landing page was reviewed, not the full PDF. Written for critical infrastructure organizations with dedicated programs, far larger than a small firm.

**Revisit when:** When CISA publishes a further edition.

## S3

**[Common Sense Guide to Mitigating Insider Threats, Seventh Edition](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/common-sense-guide-to-mitigating-insider-threats-seventh-edition/)**

Publisher: Software Engineering Institute, Carnegie Mellon University (CERT Division)

Published or updated: 2022-09-07

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Draws on a repository of more than 3,000 insider threat cases and describes 22 best practices covering both malicious and unintentional insiders. Relevant practices include Best Practice 6 (consider threats from insiders and trusted external entities), 11 (institute stringent access controls and monitoring policies), 15 (enforce separation of duties and least privilege), and 20 (develop a comprehensive workforce member termination procedure).

**Our application:** Grounds the 'access that does not match the job' paragraph and the offboarding, least privilege, and contractor points.

**Limits:** Written for organizations with security, HR, and legal functions. Practice titles were confirmed from the report PDF linked on the page; the case data comes from reported incidents, which under-represent mistakes that were never logged.

**Revisit when:** When SEI publishes an eighth edition.

## S4

**[Reducing data exfiltration by malicious insiders](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/reducing-data-exfiltration-by-malicious-insiders)**

Publisher: UK National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC)

Published or updated: Published 2022-06-30; reviewed 2026-07-29

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Recommends preventing, monitoring, and auditing: least-privilege access, improving joiners, movers, and leavers processes before buying new tools, resolving conflicts between policy and usability by co-creating processes with staff, and applying the strictest controls to privileged users and critical assets. Names email and removable storage as the highest-impact routes.

**Our application:** Supports the offboarding and least-privilege paragraph and the manager note about end dates and reviewing access.

**Limits:** Focused on malicious exfiltration and on technical, HR, and legal owners, not on what a colleague should do when asked for a favour. UK-oriented.

**Revisit when:** At the NCSC's next stated review date or if the guidance is restructured.
