# Research behind Calls That Want Something

Research reviewed: 2026-09-21. The lessons, fictional packet, hints, answer keys, and timing are original proposals. No learner trial has been performed for this course.

## S1

**[Phone Scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/phone-scams)**

Publisher: Federal Trade Commission, Consumer Advice

Published or updated: 2023-08 (month only)

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Scammers pose as government agencies, businesses, or family members, create urgency, demand hard-to-reverse payment methods, and can make any name or number show up on caller ID. The page says to hang up, never give Social Security numbers, usernames, or passwords, verify by calling a number you know is genuine, and report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

**Our application:** The lesson's list of caller personas, the point that caller ID proves nothing, and the hang-up-and-call-back habit follow this page.

**Limits:** Consumer guidance aimed at individuals, not a study of workplace vishing or of how often a callback prevents a loss. It does not describe AI voice cloning or firm payment controls.

**Revisit when:** Recheck if the FTC updates the article or changes its reporting address.

## S2

**[Scammers use AI to enhance their family emergency schemes](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/scammers-use-ai-enhance-their-family-emergency-schemes)**

Publisher: Federal Trade Commission, Consumer Alerts (Alvaro Puig)

Published or updated: 2023-03-20

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** A scammer needs only a short audio clip, which can come from content posted online, plus a voice-cloning program to imitate a voice. The alert says do not trust the voice, call the person back on a number you know is theirs, and treat requests for wire transfers, cryptocurrency, or gift cards as warning signs.

**Our application:** The lesson's claim that a familiar voice is not evidence, and the callback on a number you already have, come from this alert.

**Limits:** Written about family emergency scams, not executive impersonation at work. It does not measure how convincing cloned voices are or how often people follow the callback advice.

**Revisit when:** Recheck when the FTC publishes newer voice-cloning guidance or supersedes the alert.

## S3

**[Senior US Officials Impersonated in Malicious Messaging Campaign](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2025/PSA250515)**

Publisher: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3), Public Service Announcement

Published or updated: 2025-05-15

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Describes an ongoing vishing and smishing campaign that uses AI-generated voice messages to impersonate officials and build rapport. It says AI-generated content is often difficult to identify, advises independently identifying a phone number for the person and calling to verify, and says never to provide a two-factor code to anyone over email, text, or a messaging app.

**Our application:** Supports the lesson's treatment of cloned voices and the rule that codes are never read out to a caller. Its verify-by-independent-number advice is the callback habit.

**Limits:** Aimed at officials and their contacts rather than small firms. It describes one campaign and gives no figures on how many attempts succeeded.

**Revisit when:** Recheck when IC3 issues a newer vishing announcement.

## S4

**[Avoiding Social Engineering and Phishing Attacks](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/avoiding-social-engineering-and-phishing-attacks)**

Publisher: Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)

Published or updated: 2021-02-01 (released; the page is marked as archived content)

Reviewed: 2026-09-21

**Supports:** Defines social engineering and phishing, and vishing as the voice variant that exploits VoIP to spoof caller ID. Advises not giving out personal or organizational information unless you are certain of the person's authority, verifying a request by contacting the company directly rather than through contact details supplied with the request, and reporting to your organization's network administrators if you think you are a victim.

**Our application:** Provides the definitions and the 'verify directly, using details you already had' rule that the callback habit puts into practice, and the same-day report to IT.

**Limits:** Archived general guidance from 2021. It predates current AI voice-cloning tools and is not specific to phone requests for payments or codes.

**Revisit when:** Recheck if CISA replaces the archived page with current guidance.
