# Calls That Want Something

One short lesson on the calls and voicemails that ask for a code, a password, a payment, or remote access, and the single habit that defeats them.

Most of the calls that go wrong at a small firm do not sound like scams. They sound like the bank, a vendor, IT, or a partner in a hurry. The caller knows a few real details, the number on the screen looks right, and the request seems small.

This short module teaches one habit: end the call and call back on a number you already have. It works whether the caller is a stranger, a spoofed number, or a cloned voice, because it never asks you to judge who is telling the truth in the moment.

Work from the fictional packet on paper or in a document. Nothing here needs a tool account, and nothing asks you to phone anyone during the exercise. Try the task, compare with the example, then say out loud what you will do the next time your phone rings.

**Format:** Short module · **Track:** Security awareness · **For:** Everyone who answers a phone or joins video calls for the firm.
