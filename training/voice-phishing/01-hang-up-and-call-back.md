# 1. Hang up and call back

One habit that beats spoofed numbers, cloned voices, and pushy callers.

Suggested time: 8–10 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** A callback script for any caller, plus a note of what the D1 and D2 callers asked for.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

Voice phishing is a call or voicemail that wants something valuable: a one-time code, a password, a payment approval, new bank details, or remote access. The caller claims to be the bank, a vendor, IT, or an executive, and wants it now. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S4](sources.md#s4)

Caller ID proves nothing; it can be made to show any name or number. The voice proves nothing either: a short clip from a webinar or greeting is enough to clone someone. 'It sounded exactly like her' now describes the attack. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S2](sources.md#s2), [S3](sources.md#s3), [S5](sources.md#s5)

So I do not judge the caller in the moment. I say, 'I am going to hang up and call you back through the main number,' then do it using the directory, the card, or a statement. I report the call either way. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S2](sources.md#s2), [S4](sources.md#s4)

## Worked example: Turning a demand into a callback

**Before**

Caller ID matches the bank, the caller knows about the flagged transactions, and the clock is running. I read out the code so the charges get reversed in time.

**After**

I say: 'I cannot read codes over the phone. I will hang up and call the fraud line on the back of the card.' I note the time, number shown, and request, then make that call and report to IT.

**Why:** The code is what lets someone into the account; a genuine fraud team does not need it. Calling back on a number you already have moves the conversation to a line the caller cannot control.

**Deliverable:** A short script and two one-line notes, ready to send to IT.

- [ ] The script ends the call without arguing about who the caller is.
- [ ] The callback number comes from the directory, card, or statement, not the caller.
- [ ] Each note names the request: code, password, payment, or remote access.

## Check one decision

The D2 voicemail sounds exactly like Priya and names a real matter. What do you do?

- **A.** Text the loaner number to confirm, then approve the wire if the reply says yes.
- **B.** Leave the wire in the queue, call Priya's directory number, and report the voicemail to IT.
- **C.** Approve the wire, since a cloned voice could not know about the closing, and tell Priya later.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **B**.

**A:** A reply from the loaner only proves whoever left the voicemail holds that phone. It is the channel the caller chose, and D3 rules out payment approvals by voicemail or text, whoever asks.

**B:** This is the habit. The wire keeps its two approvers, the callback uses a number you already had, and IT gets the details whether or not Priya really called.

**C:** A real detail is not proof; the matter name could come from a compromised mailbox or a public filing. D3 bars payment approvals by phone, even from a partner, and a sent wire cannot be recalled.

</details>
