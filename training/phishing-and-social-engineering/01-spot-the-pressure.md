# 1. Spot the pressure

Read a message for its ask, then for the pressure that stops you checking.

Suggested time: 15–20 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** Mark up two messages with their specific signals and say what each wants the reader to do.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

I read a message twice. The first pass asks only what it wants me to do: open a link, open a file, send money, reply, or approve a prompt. Every lure has an ask. Then comes the pressure: a deadline, a senior name, a request for secrecy. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S2](sources.md#s2)

Identity next. A display name is free text; the address after the @ sign is what was actually used. In a link only the registered domain counts, whatever familiar name sits in front of it. Lookalikes add a word or swap a letter. [S4](sources.md#s4)

A well-written message is not a safe message. Guidance still lists spelling mistakes as a sign, and they do appear, but their absence proves nothing. A genuine notice like D3 differs in what it asks through itself: nothing. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S4](sources.md#s4)

## Worked example: Marking up the document notice

**Before**

A Harbourline notice about the Ridgeway binder, like every week. The sender name matches and the link has our name in it, so I opened it.

**After**

Ask: sign in with my work password. Pressure: a 24-hour expiry and the managing partner's name. Identity: the address is share@harbourline-docs.example.net and the link's registered domain is docs-view.example.net; D4 says our only domain is harbourline.example.com. Missing: any reason Priya would send me this.

**Why:** It names the ask and the pressure first, then checks the address and registered domain against D4 instead of trusting a display name.

## Check one decision

Which reading of D2 is best supported by the packet?

- **A.** It is genuinely from Priya, because it knows about the Ridgeway closing and Dana's role; the payment simply needs Dana's approval.
- **B.** The unknown number, secrecy, the deadline, and a new payee are the signals; the message wants Sam to confirm availability so account details can follow.
- **C.** It is harmless for now because it only asks Sam to confirm he is at his desk; replying costs nothing.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **B**.

**A:** Knowing a deal and a colleague's name is not identity; D2 offers nothing verifiable. The unknown number, secrecy, deadline, and new account are all present, and D4 says a text never authorises a payment.

**B:** This names the ask and each pressure signal and treats the claimed name as unverified. What to do next is Lesson 2.

**C:** Replying tells the sender the number is live and opens the channel for the account details; the payment ask is already in the message. D4 says never reply.

</details>
