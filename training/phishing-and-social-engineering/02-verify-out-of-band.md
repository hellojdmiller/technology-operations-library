# 2. Verify out of band

Report first, then check through a channel the message did not supply.

Suggested time: 15–20 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** Write a verification plan for one message that a colleague could follow unaided.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

I report first. The report button in D4 gets the message to people who can check every other inbox. It takes ten seconds and costs nothing if I am wrong. Microsoft and Google both put reporting one click away inside the message. [S4](sources.md#s4), [S5](sources.md#s5)

Then I verify through a channel the message did not supply: a directory number, a colleague in person, a website I type. The FTC's rule: a phone number or website you know is real, not the information in the message. I never reply or use its links. [S2](sources.md#s2), [S1](sources.md#s1)

Invites and shared-document notices get the same care. They arrive as system mail, carry links, and accepting or declining an invite sends a reply. I open documents from the portal I type and leave unexpected invites for the technology team. D3 gets verified too.

## Worked example: A verification plan for the text message

**Before**

I will call the number that texted me and ask something only Priya would know. If she knows about Ridgeway, I will wait for the details.

**After**

Report: screenshot the text to phishing@harbourline.example.com. Verify: call Priya on her directory extension or the number in my phone; if unreachable, tell Dana in person. Do not: reply or call the new number. Outcome: even if Priya confirms, the payment still needs Dana's approval under D4.

**Why:** Every channel comes from the directory or the room, not the message. Reporting comes first, and the payment stays with D4 whatever the call reveals.

## Check one decision

Sam wants to check D1 quickly. Which action fits D4?

- **A.** Click the link to see where it goes, but do not enter a password.
- **B.** Reply to the notice and ask who shared the document.
- **C.** Use the Report button, then ask Priya in person or by extension whether she shared anything, and open documents only from the portal you type.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **C**.

**A:** Clicking is the ask, and a page can capture a session or run code without a password. The link's registered domain is docs-view.example.net, not the firm's; D4 says never click.

**B:** The reply goes to share@harbourline-docs.example.net, a lookalike domain, and tells the sender the address is live. D4 says never reply; the question belongs to Priya at her desk.

**C:** Reporting first gets the message to the team, the channels come from the directory and the room, and the typed portal is the only safe way to open a real share.

</details>
