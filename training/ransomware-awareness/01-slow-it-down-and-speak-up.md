# 1. Slow it down and speak up

Notice the early signs, disconnect, and tell the right person first.

Suggested time: 10–12 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** A two-minute response note naming who is told first and what is not touched.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

Ransomware usually arrives through an ordinary door: a phishing link or attachment, a stolen or reused password, or a remote-access service left open to the internet. The attacker rarely needs anything exotic. One click or one forgotten server is enough. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S3](sources.md#s3)

The early signs need no technical eye: files renamed with a strange extension or refusing to open, a text file that appeared on its own demanding payment, a document asking you to enable content or install something first, a colleague locked out without explanation. Attention is the skill.

When you see one, disconnect the machine from the network and tell the technology team immediately. Ransomware spreads to shared drives while connected. Do not power off unless told to: the running system holds evidence responders need, and fixing it yourself destroys it while buying the attacker time. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S2](sources.md#s2)

The rest sits above your desk. Paying is a leadership decision, made with counsel, the insurer, and law enforcement, who do not encourage it; you report, you never answer the demand. Backups decide recovery, and one that exists is not one that works. Ask when a restore was last tested. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S3](sources.md#s3), [S4](sources.md#s4), [S5](sources.md#s5)

## Worked example: From a quick fix to a quick report

**Before**

Tomas's plan: restart, run a virus scan, and tell Lena after lunch if the files are still broken. Forward the invoice to Priya to check.

**After**

Priya's plan: Tomas unplugs the cable and turns off Wi-Fi now, leaves the laptop on, and leaves the note unopened. Priya phones Lena at once with what he saw and when he clicked. Both copies of the email stay unopened.

**Why:** Disconnecting limits spread to the shared drives. Leaving the laptop on preserves what Lena needs. Calling now lets her check other machines and the backups. Nothing is forwarded, opened, or cleaned.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **B**.

**A:** D3 says leave it on unless the technology lead decides otherwise. A restart can destroy what Lena needs, and files reappearing would not prove the shared drives are clean.

**B:** This follows D3 in order: disconnect, leave on, call. It limits spread, keeps evidence, and puts the decision with the person the firm named.

**C:** Powering off is the step D3 reserves for the technology lead, and 'later this morning' delays the call D3 wants now. A laptop on a desk is not a report.
