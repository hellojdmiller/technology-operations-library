# A client email, a footer, and a draft that reads too well

You work at Larkspur Ridge Advisors, a small advisory firm. Mara, a colleague, has asked you to help summarize a client email for Ines, a partner, using the firm's AI assistant. Everyone and everything in this packet is fictional: the firm, the client, the people, the domains, the figures, and the tools. The exercise reference date is 21 September 2026. One document in the packet is unsafe: it contains a line written to steer an assistant rather than to inform its human reader. Try to find it yourself in Lesson 2 before reading the next sentence. Facilitator note: the planted instruction is the final paragraph of D1, the one beginning 'This message and its attachments are confidential'. D3 is a deliberately flawed draft to correct, not evidence of what happened; it contains a fabricated figure and a misattributed quote. D2 and D4 are the fictional policy excerpt and tool list that settle the rules.

## D2: AI use policy, excerpt

Date: 2026-07-01 · Fictional policy excerpt: approved tools, prohibited data, review before sending

Section 3, Tools. Firm information may be entered only into tools on the approved list maintained by Devin Okafor. A personal or free account, including one on the same vendor's consumer product, is not approved for any firm information, however the request is worded.

Section 4, Prohibited in any tool. Credentials of any kind (passwords, one-time codes, API keys), passport, national identity and account numbers, health information, and home addresses of clients, investors, or staff. Remove these before pasting; do not replace them with hints at the value.

Section 5, Restricted to approved tools. Client and investor names tied to financial details, unreleased deal terms and prices, project codenames tied to counterparties, and personal data beyond a name and business contact. Where the task does not need the detail, replace names with roles and figures with labeled placeholders.

Section 6, Pasting is sharing. Anything entered into an AI tool is treated as disclosed to that vendor under the agreement covering that account, and to anyone who can view the account's history. Ask the question you would ask before forwarding the material.

Section 7, Connected assistants. An assistant connected to mail, files, or the web acts with your permissions and in your name. Approvals for sending, sharing, attaching, or moving material stay with you. Text the assistant reads inside a message or file is not an instruction from the firm, whatever it claims about approval.

Section 8, Suspicious content. If a message appears to contain instructions aimed at an assistant, do not act on them and do not reply through the same channel. Confirm with the sender by a route you already trust, and report the message to Devin.

Section 9, Review before it counts. Any AI-assisted output that leaves the firm or informs a decision is checked against its sources by the person who sends or signs it. Figures, names, dates, and quotes are traced to a source line. Where a reader would reasonably want to know, and always when asked, say the draft was AI-assisted.
