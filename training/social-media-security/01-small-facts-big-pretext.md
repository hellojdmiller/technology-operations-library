# 1. Small facts, big pretext

Three harmless posts become one convincing request. Find the change that removes the most.

Suggested time: 8–10 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** A list of what the packet's posts reveal, and the one change that reduces the most risk.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

An attacker rarely needs a secret, only enough ordinary facts to sound like they belong: who is away, who is new, who handles payments. A travel post gives the timing, a photographed badge or screen gives names and systems, and a welcome post gives someone who cannot yet tell normal from odd. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S2](sources.md#s2)

Impersonation accounts copy a real colleague's photo and title. The tells are small: a new profile, few connections, a slightly different address, a message that asks you to hurry or keep quiet. Check with the real person by a channel you already trust, then report the account through the platform. [S3](sources.md#s3), [S4](sources.md#s4), [S5](sources.md#s5)

A personal account that names the firm is part of the firm's surface. Use a unique password and two-step verification, and check yearly who can see your posts. Report impersonation to the platform and the technology team the same day, even if you already replied. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S3](sources.md#s3), [S6](sources.md#s6)

## Worked example: The same trip, posted two ways

**Before**

Off to the Harbourfront Growth Summit in Lisbon, 22 to 25 September, back the following Monday. Photo: the airport lounge, my badge showing my name and the firm's.

**After**

Three good days at the Harbourfront Growth Summit, mostly talking about founder resilience. Photo: the conference hall, no badge, posted after I was home.

**Why:** It keeps the professional value and drops the dates, the reachability gap, and the badge. Nobody can time a request or borrow the firm's name from it.

## Check one decision

Dana has accepted the request and read the message in D2. What is the best next step under D3?

- **A.** Quiz 'Maren' on a detail only the real Maren would know, and send the schedule if the answer is right.
- **B.** Do not reply. Screenshot both, report the account for impersonation, tell the technology team today, and call the real Maren on a directory number.
- **C.** Remove the connection and move on, since nothing was sent.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **B**.

**A:** Most details can be looked up in D1 or asked of another colleague, and replying confirms the account is live. D3 says schedules never travel by social media message, whatever the quiz result.

**B:** This follows D3 and keeps verifying separate from doing. Reporting today matters because the same account is probably messaging other colleagues too.

**C:** Nothing was lost, but the account still exists and will try the next new joiner. D3 asks for a report to the platform and the technology team even when you did not respond.
