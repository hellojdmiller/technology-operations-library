# 1. Six habits, one inbox

Six checks that catch the message that matters, on a suspicious email and a hijacked mailbox.

Suggested time: 8–10 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** A personal six-habit checklist with one packet example under each habit.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

The display name says Owen; the address says otherwise. I read the address before acting on any message and compare it with the directory. A senior name plus a deadline earns a closer look, not a faster one. [S3](sources.md#s3), [S7](sources.md#s7)

Hover, or long-press on a phone, to see where a link goes before opening it. An unexpected attachment is a question for the sender through a channel I already trust. 'Cannot take calls' is pressure, not proof. [S1](sources.md#s1), [S7](sources.md#s7)

Sending has quiet mistakes too. Auto-complete picks the wrong Priya, Reply All reaches forty people, and a forwarded thread carries history its new reader was never meant to see. I check recipients and trim history first.

The Report button does what deletion cannot: it hands IT the evidence and tells the mail provider; the message leaves the inbox anyway. Report without blame and the firm sees the campaign; delete quietly and it sees nothing. [S2](sources.md#s2), [S4](sources.md#s4), [S7](sources.md#s7)

Rules and forwards turn a stolen password into a long-term listener. Attackers create rules in mailboxes they enter, and owners rarely notice. I check mine monthly and keep work mail out of personal accounts, beyond the firm's protections. [S6](sources.md#s6), [S5](sources.md#s5), [S8](sources.md#s8)

## Worked example: The same message, read twice

**Before**

Owen needs the template by 10:00, so I open the zip, update the bank details, and reply.

**After**

Owen's name over fastmail-relay.example.net, an [EXTERNAL] tag on a message about our own bank account, an unexpected zip, a link to a domain that is not ours. I open neither; I report it and call Owen's directory number.

**Why:** Every habit fired on one message, and reporting kept the evidence. Calling the directory number separates deciding from doing.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **B**.

**A:** Deleting the rule destroys the evidence of what left since 17 September, and a password change before IT checks may leave other footholds. D3: report today, keep the rule, change the password when IT confirms.

**B:** This follows D3 and the takeover guidance: a rule the owner did not create signals compromise, IT needs the evidence intact, and outside contact follows IT's assessment. The suppliers' questions confirm the diversion.

**C:** The rule also forwards every invoice to docsafe-mail.example.net, outside the firm, and two suppliers are already asking. Nothing lost inside does not mean nothing left; D3 calls that a possible takeover.
