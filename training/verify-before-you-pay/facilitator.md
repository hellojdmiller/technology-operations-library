# Verify Before You Pay: facilitator guide

A colleague can run this course as one session of about 60–85 minutes, or as 3 short sessions. No specialist background is required to facilitate; the answer keys are in each lesson.

## Before the session

- Send or print the [fictional packet](source-packet.md) and the [worksheet](worksheet.md).
- Say plainly that everyone and everything in the packet is fictional, and that no real client, investor, employee, or credential data is used or discussed.
- Agree how answers will be shared: spoken, written, or in pairs. Reading and response alternatives are welcome.

## Ground rules

- Try the task before opening the example answer. The example is a comparison, not a mark scheme.
- Explaining a choice matters more than matching the wording.
- A first attempt with honest gaps is more useful than a polished copy of the example.

## Lesson 1: Recognize business email compromise (15–20 minutes)

**Outcome:** Mark up two payment-related messages with what is being changed and what is creating the pressure.

**Run it:** read the explanation together or ask participants to read it, walk through the worked example, then give the task. Allow most of the time for the task and the comparison.

**Discussion prompt:** Which detail in D2 convinced you most, and why does it say nothing about where the money would go?

**Changed case:** Suppose D1 had three spelling mistakes and came from an address one letter off the vendor record. Does your markup change?

<details>
<summary>Changed-case answer</summary>

The clues get easier; the test does not change. The destination moved and a deadline is pushing, so mark and verify it the same way. Train yourself on typos and the clean message from the real mailbox gets through.

</details>

**Check:** D2 came from Marcus's real address inside a thread you remember. What does that tell you? The supported choice is **B**.

<details>
<summary>Why the other options fall short</summary>

**A:** A hijacked mailbox replies inside real threads from the real address. In this packet the earlier D2 messages are genuine; the final one adds a new payee, drops Priya, and sets a cutoff. That is what needs verifying.

**C:** Partners at small firms email about payments constantly, and D2 could be a hurried genuine request. The decision is not 'scam or not' from the header; it is 'verify the new destination before anything moves'.

</details>

## Lesson 2: Verify through a second channel (15–20 minutes)

**Outcome:** Write a verification plan for one change request that names the channel, the person, and the record kept.

**Run it:** read the explanation together or ask participants to read it, walk through the worked example, then give the task. Allow most of the time for the task and the comparison.

**Discussion prompt:** Which line of your plan would fail if the attacker also controlled Priya's mailbox, and what would you change?

**Changed case:** Northfield's accounts line goes to voicemail. Two minutes later Ben emails offering a video call right now to confirm.

<details>
<summary>Changed-case answer</summary>

A video call arranged through the same thread is the same channel wearing a headset, and video can be imitated too. Leave a voicemail, try the switchboard number on the record, and wait. If the deadline arrives first, pay the old details.

</details>

**Check:** The D3 caller said not to call back and to email Marcus instead. What is the supported move? The supported choice is **C**.

<details>
<summary>Why the other options fall short</summary>

**A:** A cloned voice needs a short sample, and knowing the invoice shows the caller read the same emails you did. In this packet the caller also supplied the reason not to verify.

**B:** That is the channel the request chose for you. In this packet Marcus's mailbox already carries D2, which itself needs verifying, so a reply from it settles nothing.

</details>

## Lesson 3: Design the procedure and the response (15–20 minutes)

**Outcome:** Draft a one-page payment-change procedure and first-hour response that a small firm could adopt.

**Run it:** read the explanation together or ask participants to read it, walk through the worked example, then give the task. Allow most of the time for the task and the comparison.

**Discussion prompt:** Which step would the busiest partner most want to skip, and what will you say when they ask?

**Changed case:** The firm has three people. The finance lead is also a partner and today the only approver. What changes?

<details>
<summary>Changed-case answer</summary>

Roles stay separate even when the people do not. Whoever receives a request cannot be its sole approver, so the third person verifies by callback and the partner approves. If only two are available, the change waits. The bank's dual-authorization feature can be the second control.

</details>

**Check:** On Monday at 10:40 Dana learns Friday's $240,000 wire from D2 went to an unverified account. What comes first? The supported choice is **B**.

<details>
<summary>Why the other options fall short</summary>

**A:** Worth knowing, but it uses the mailbox that may still be compromised and spends minutes the recall needs. In this packet who sent D2 does not change the first action.

**C:** Deleting destroys what the bank, the technology team, and law enforcement will ask for, and quiet investigation is the delay the no-blame rule exists to prevent. The thread holds the account details the bank needs.

</details>
