# 3. Recover safely

Lockouts and suspected compromise are when you are most persuadable. Know where your codes are, verify support yourself, report early.

Suggested time: 15–20 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** A short recovery note for one scenario, including who to call and what not to do.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

Recovery is when you are most persuadable. You are locked out, or suspect someone is in, and want it fixed in ten minutes. Attackers know this, so the recovery path is where they impersonate support and add a deadline. Slowing down is the skill.

Know where your recovery codes are before you need them. D3 5.1 puts work-account codes in the password manager as secure notes and keeps the manager's printed kit at home. Codes in email, chat, or a photo belong to whoever has your mailbox.

Two patterns recur. Support impersonation asks for a code or an approval. SIM swap runs the other way: the criminal impersonates you to your carrier, takes your number, and receives the text codes that reset your passwords. The FBI's warning sign is a phone with no service. [S2](sources.md#s2), [S5](sources.md#s5)

Tell the technology team early. Early reports stay small, and D3 6.2 promises no penalty for a false alarm. Verify support requests by calling the published extension yourself, never a number in the message: under D3 6.1 the team never asks for a code.

## Worked example: From here is the code to I will call

**Before**

Priya's draft reply to D4, 08:46: "OK, sorry, I am just nervous. The code is 482913. Approving the prompt now."

**After**

Priya's revised reply: "I cannot share codes or approve prompts. I will call the technology team on the directory extension now." She sends nothing else, phones 4400, and tells Lena what was asked.

**Why:** D3 6.1: the team never asks for a code, so anyone who does is not the team. Verification moves to a channel Priya chose. A lockout is an inconvenience; the code is a takeover.

## Check one decision

D4 says the account locks for 48 hours unless Priya pastes the code now. Which response fits D3?

- **A.** Paste the code: IT already knows about the overnight sign-ins.
- **B.** Refuse, approve nothing, and call the technology team on the directory extension.
- **C.** Ask the sender to prove they are IT with a staff ID first.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **B**.

**A:** Knowing about the prompts proves nothing; D2 shows the attacker caused them. D3 6.1 says the team never asks for a code, and D4's sender is an external guest account.

**B:** This fits D3 6.1 and 6.2: the request is the tell, verification moves to a channel Priya looked up, and the early call lets the team act on D2 too.

**C:** An ID image can be copied or invented, and replying keeps Priya on the attacker's channel and clock. D3 6.1 gives one method: call the published extension. D4's deadline is the pressure tactic.

</details>
