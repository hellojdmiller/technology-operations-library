## D1: Device standard, excerpt

Date: 2026-06-01 · Fictional policy excerpt: what any device that touches firm information must meet

Scope. This standard applies to any device that opens firm mail, chat, files, or client information, whether the firm or the person owns it. The technology team (contact: Wen) maintains it and answers questions about it.

Updates. Operating system and browser updates are installed within seven days of release, and automatic updates are turned on. A device its maker no longer supports with updates is not used for firm information.

Lock and unlock. Devices lock automatically after no more than five minutes idle. Phones use a passcode of at least six digits; laptops use a strong password or passphrase. Fingerprint or face unlock is welcome as a convenience on top, never as the only unlock.

Encryption. Full disk encryption is on: FileVault on Mac, Device Encryption or BitLocker on Windows, and the built-in encryption that comes with a passcode on a modern phone. For managed devices the technology team holds the recovery key.

Managed and personal devices. Firm files and client documents live only on firm-managed laptops. A personal phone may run the firm's mail and chat apps if it is enrolled in the firm's app management, which lets the team remove firm data without touching personal photos, messages, or apps.

Approved apps. Anything that stores firm or client information must be on the approved app list kept by the technology team. Request a new tool through the tool request form; the team answers within five working days and will suggest an approved alternative if the answer is no.

Lost or stolen devices. Report to the technology team immediately, at any hour, by phone or chat, before anything else. The team will remotely lock or wipe the device. Do not attempt to recover the device yourself.
