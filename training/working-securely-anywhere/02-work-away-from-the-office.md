# 2. Work away from the office

Use someone else's network without fear, and know the first call when a device goes missing.

Suggested time: 15–20 minutes. Work on paper or in a document.

**Outcome:** Write a travel plan for one trip and a lost-device message that puts the report first.

Read the [shared fictional packet](source-packet.md). Keep it available throughout the lesson.

Public Wi-Fi is less dangerous than it used to be. The FTC notes that most websites now encrypt traffic, so connecting through a public network is usually safe; look for the lock or https in the address bar. Our mail, files, and chat use it too. [S4](sources.md#s4)

Remaining risks are quieter: a portal asking for more than it needs, a lookalike network copying the hotel's name, and someone reading your screen. I prefer my hotspot or the firm's VPN, give a portal only what the desk card says, and never type a password into a page that popped up.

Also: back to a wall, print nothing. Border officers may lawfully inspect devices, so I carry only what the trip needs and keep files in the firm's cloud rather than on the disk. If a device goes missing, I report first; lock and wipe only start once the team knows.

## Worked example: Report first, then look

**Before**

I'll wait for the lounge desk before I tell Wen. It's probably fine and I don't want to make a fuss.

**After**

To Wen, from the gate: 'Firm MacBook left in the Concourse B lounge around 14:10, lid closed, probably locked, Hartwell folder and mail open. Please lock or wipe it. Lounge desk is looking; will update.'

**Why:** One message, easily reversed if the laptop turns up; waiting cannot be reversed. The team gets time, place, what was open, and permission to act before the phone dies.

## Check one decision

Ines has 20 minutes until boarding and the lounge desk is searching. What is the right next step under D1?

- **A.** Wait until boarding to see if the lounge finds it, then report if not.
- **B.** Report to Wen now with time, place, and what was open, then keep asking the lounge desk.
- **C.** Use Find My to locate the laptop and go back to recover it herself.

<details>
<summary>Answer and feedback</summary>

The supported choice is **B**.

**A:** D1 puts the report before anything else. The Hartwell folder and mail were open, and at 8 percent battery a delayed report may not happen for hours; no wipe can start until it does.

**B:** This follows D1's order: report first, then search. It can be sent from the gate, gives the team what it needs, and is easily reversed if the laptop turns up.

**C:** D1 says not to attempt recovery yourself and puts the report first. Chasing the laptop on a dying phone risks the flight and leaves the team unaware; finding a device does not protect its data.
