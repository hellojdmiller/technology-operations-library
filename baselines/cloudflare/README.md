# Cloudflare: a baseline for technology operations

I would treat Cloudflare as the place where the firm's name on the internet is decided: which registrar holds the domain, which DNS records answer, which certificate visitors see, who can reach an internal tool, and which member or token can change any of it. A small firm often puts all of that into one account with one or two administrators, so the account itself becomes the most valuable thing to protect. A takeover there is quieter than a lost laptop and can redirect mail, the website, and every SaaS verification record at once.

This guide is a proposed operating standard, not a certification. [Twenty control assertions](desired-state.json), the [evidence worksheet](evidence-worksheet.csv), and a [risk assessment template](risk-assessment.csv) make it reviewable. They are not settings to import. The linked Cloudflare documentation was reviewed on **2026-09-20**; each control carries its own source and review gate. The [local assessment tool](../README.md) compares what a reviewer asserts against these targets. It does not sign in to an account, read a zone, or verify evidence.

## Make the scope explicit

Before rating any setting, record the account and its members, the plan of every zone (Free, Pro, Business, or Enterprise), the Zero Trust plan and seat count, purchased add-ons such as Advanced Certificate Manager, Bot Management, or Logpush, every hostname with its proxied or DNS-only status, every origin and how it connects, the internal applications behind Access, the devices running WARP, the identity provider, and where each domain is registered. Keep the actual details outside this repository.

Plan dependencies matter more here than on most platforms. Several controls describe a capability that exists on every plan, several describe one that needs Pro or Business, and a few (zone holds, Logpush, Bot Management, registry lock through Custom Domain Protection) are Enterprise features. Each control's `review_gate` names the dependency I found in the documentation. A missing entitlement is a budget or compensating-control decision; it does not make the risk absent, so leave the control unknown or record a gap rather than marking it not applicable.

Use three example populations when designing a pilot: an administrator who changes DNS and security settings, an investment professional who reaches an internal tool through Access while travelling, and an external developer or agency that maintains the website through an API token. The purpose is to expose a business dependency before an account-wide restriction does.

## Registrar and DNS: controls 01 to 04

The registrar decision is the root of everything else. Cloudflare's transfer-out procedure shows that anyone with super-admin or admin permission for a zone can unlock a domain and read its authorization code, and that a pending transfer auto-approves on the fifth day if nobody acts. I would keep the lock on, keep auto-renew on with a payment method somebody monitors, and make unlocking a two-person event in practice. [Transfer domain out from Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/registrar/account-options/transfer-out-from-cloudflare/), [Domain management](https://developers.cloudflare.com/registrar/account-options/domain-management/). Registry lock with out-of-band verification is sold as [Custom Domain Protection](https://developers.cloudflare.com/registrar/custom-domain-protection/) for Enterprise accounts only.

DNSSEC is free on every plan and, for domains registered with Cloudflare, the DS record reaches the registry automatically over one to two days. For domains held elsewhere the DS must be added at that registrar by hand. Confirm validation with an external resolver rather than trusting the dashboard status alone. [DNSSEC](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/dnssec/), [Enable DNSSEC at Cloudflare Registrar](https://developers.cloudflare.com/registrar/get-started/enable-dnssec/).

Record change control is mostly discipline. Comments work on all plans and tags need Pro or above; both are visible only to members and do not affect resolution, which makes them a cheap way to record an owner and purpose. Pair that with a dated zone export stored outside the account and a habit of reading DNS changes in the audit log. [DNS record comments and tags](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/manage-dns-records/reference/record-attributes/), [Import and export DNS records](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/manage-dns-records/how-to/import-and-export/). Zone holds, which stop another account from adding a zone you already run, are Enterprise only and on by default there. [Zone holds](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/account/account-security/zone-holds/).

The email control looks at the DNS host's side of sender authentication, including parked domains that never send mail. The record values come from the mail provider's documentation, never from an example; the [Google Workspace](../google-workspace/README.md) and [Microsoft 365](../microsoft-365/README.md) baselines cover the provider side. DMARC Management is available on all plans with Cloudflare DNS and gives report visibility without setting policy. [Email records](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/manage-dns-records/how-to/email-records/), [DMARC Management](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dmarc-management/). Cloudflare Email Security is a separate inbound-protection product with its own deployment modes and is not required by this baseline. [Email Security](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/email-security/).

## Account security: controls 05 to 08

Cloudflare's model gives the Super Administrator role everything, including member management, billing, and account-owned tokens. Only Super Administrators can invite, edit, or revoke members. I would keep that role with two or three named operators, use domain-scoped roles and read-only roles for everyone else, and test a delegated task before taking a broad role away. Resource-scoped roles are in beta. [Account member roles](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/manage-members/roles/), [Manage account members](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/manage-members/manage/).

A Super Administrator can turn on 2FA Enforcement for all members. Cloudflare's methods include authenticator apps, security keys, and email codes; email is the weakest and I would not accept it for administrators. Generate backup codes, test one, and keep them out of the assessment record. Dashboard SSO is free on every plan but needs a verified mail domain and a Zero Trust organization with an identity provider already configured. [Two-factor authentication](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/user-profiles/2fa/), [Dashboard SSO](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/manage-members/dashboard-sso/).

API tokens are where small firms leak the most privilege. A token is permanent unless an end date is set, the Global API Key carries the user's full permissions and cannot be scoped or expired, and a user token dies with the user. For a durable automation I would create an account-owned token (Super Administrator only, `cfat_` prefix) with the minimum permissions and zone scope, a client IP restriction where the caller is fixed, and an expiry. Account tokens do not yet cover every product, including Registrar. [Create API token](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/api/get-started/create-token/), [Restrict API tokens](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/api/how-to/restrict-tokens/), [API keys](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/api/get-started/keys/), [Account-owned API tokens](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/api/get-started/account-owned-tokens/).

Audit logs are kept for 18 months on every plan. The version 2 dashboard shows the most recent 90 days; the API returns the full window and Logpush export is Enterprise, with separate jobs for version 1 and 2. Decide the retention the firm actually needs, then prove the log works by making a synthetic change and finding it. [Audit Logs version 2](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/account/account-security/audit-logs/), [Review audit logs version 1](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/account/account-security/review-audit-logs/).

## Edge protection: controls 09 to 11

The WAF only sees traffic to proxied hostnames; a DNS-only record bypasses it. The Free plan receives the Free Managed Ruleset; the Cloudflare Managed Ruleset and the OWASP Core Ruleset need Pro or above. I would deploy what the plan allows in a logging action first, read the events for a week, then switch to blocking with documented exceptions. [WAF Managed Rules](https://developers.cloudflare.com/waf/managed-rules/).

Administrative paths deserve their own rule. Zone Lockdown restricts a URL to allowlisted addresses, with 3, 10, or 200 rules on Pro, Business, and Enterprise and none on Free. An IP allowlist fails the moment an administrator travels, so I would put the path behind Access instead, or in addition, and test the legitimate administrator path before enforcing. [Zone Lockdown](https://developers.cloudflare.com/waf/tools/zone-lockdown/), [Self-hosted Access applications](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/access-controls/applications/http-apps/self-hosted-public-app/).

Bot protection and rate limiting are grouped as one abuse control because a small firm usually configures them together and both can break a legitimate integration. Bot Fight Mode on Free applies to all traffic and cannot exclude a client; Super Bot Fight Mode on Pro and Business adds allow, block, and challenge choices; Bot Management is an Enterprise add-on. Rate limiting allows 1, 2, 5, or 100 rules by plan and counters lag by a few seconds, so it is not a hard ceiling. Test a monitoring probe and a partner API call after enabling either. [Bot protection plans](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/plans/), [Bot Fight Mode](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/plans/free/), [Super Bot Fight Mode](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/plans/pro/), [Rate limiting rules](https://developers.cloudflare.com/waf/rate-limiting-rules/).

## TLS and origin: controls 12 to 14

Minimum TLS Version and HSTS are available on every plan. Raise the minimum to 1.2 after checking for legacy clients. Enable HSTS only after confirming that every subdomain in scope serves HTTPS, because turning HTTPS off later leaves visitors locked out for the max-age period; preload needs a 12-month max-age and is hard to reverse. Per-hostname TLS settings need Advanced Certificate Manager. [Minimum TLS Version](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/edge-certificates/additional-options/minimum-tls/), [HTTP Strict Transport Security](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/edge-certificates/additional-options/http-strict-transport-security/).

Between Cloudflare and the origin, Full (strict) validates that the origin certificate is unexpired, matches the hostname, and comes from a public CA or Cloudflare Origin CA. Origin CA certificates are free on every plan but are trusted only by Cloudflare and receive no expiry notifications, so somebody has to track the date. A mismatch produces 526 errors; test one hostname before switching modes. [Full (strict) encryption mode](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/origin-configuration/ssl-modes/full-strict/), [Origin CA certificates](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/origin-configuration/origin-ca/).

None of the edge controls matter if the origin answers directly. Cloudflare's own guidance ranks Cloudflare Tunnel first because the origin has no inbound listener, then Authenticated Origin Pulls at the transport layer, then a Cloudflare IP allowlist at the network layer, and it recommends rotating origin IPs after onboarding because historical DNS is public. Authenticated Origin Pulls does not work with Tunnel, and the global certificate proves only that a request came from Cloudflare, not from your account. The acceptance test is a request sent straight to the origin address that is refused. [Cloudflare Tunnel](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/networks/connectors/cloudflare-tunnel/), [Authenticated Origin Pulls](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/origin-configuration/authenticated-origin-pull/), [Protect your origin server](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/security/protect-your-origin-server/).

## Zero Trust: controls 15 to 18

Zero Trust is licensed per seat: a user consumes a seat on first authentication and releases it when removed from the organization. The Free plan has a user cap that the developer documentation I reviewed does not state, so confirm it on Cloudflare's plan page before relying on it. [Seat management](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/team-and-resources/users/seat-management/), [Account limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/account-limits/).

Access applications are deny by default; a user must match an Allow policy. Bypass disables Access entirely for matching requests and does not log them, so every Bypass needs a documented reason. Policies are reusable across applications, which means editing one can change access to several; use the policy tester for a named leaver and a named authorized user before and after a change, and have the origin validate the Access token so a direct request cannot skip the check. [Access policies](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/access-controls/policies/), [Access policy management](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/access-controls/policies/policy-management/).

Access is only as good as the identity behind it. Cloudflare integrates with generic SAML and OIDC providers and can run several at once. One-time PIN is no longer added automatically but can be configured alongside an identity provider, which lets a user choose the weaker path; I would keep it off for staff applications. A removed user shows as inactive and reclaims a seat if they authenticate again, so leaver testing means confirming denial, not just deletion. [Identity provider integration](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/integrations/identity-providers/), [One-time PIN login](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/integrations/identity-providers/one-time-pin/).

Device posture needs the WARP client on the device. The client checks include disk encryption, operating system version, firewall, domain join, and file or application presence; results are polled every five minutes by default, Gateway caches them and can take up to 15 minutes to enforce a change, and a failed check does not end an active Gateway session. Test a deliberately non-compliant device and record the denial. [Device posture](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/reusable-components/posture-checks/), [Client posture checks](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/reusable-components/posture-checks/client-checks/).

Gateway DNS filtering works at the lookup stage across every application, so blocking the security categories is the highest-value, lowest-friction start. HTTP filtering needs the Cloudflare root certificate or a custom certificate installed on devices and is a decision about inspecting staff traffic that the firm should approve explicitly. Third-party VPNs and applications with hard-coded fallback resolvers bypass filtering; client block notifications need Enterprise. [Gateway DNS policies](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/traffic-policies/dns-policies/), [Gateway HTTP policies](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/traffic-policies/http-policies/).

## Alerting, export, and recovery: controls 19 to 20

Notifications go by email on every plan, by webhook from Pro, and to PagerDuty from Business. Universal SSL and HTTP DDoS alerts exist on all plans; Security Events alerts need Business or Enterprise; advanced security, bot detection, and Logpush job alerts are Enterprise. Route them to a shared destination with an owner and test delivery, because a notification aimed at a departed person's mailbox is the same as none. Logpush for zone logs and for Zero Trust logs is Enterprise and cannot backfill, so the export decision has to precede the incident. [Cloudflare Notifications](https://developers.cloudflare.com/notifications/), [Available notifications](https://developers.cloudflare.com/notifications/notification-available/), [Logpush](https://developers.cloudflare.com/logs/logpush/), [Logpush for Zero Trust logs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/insights/logs/logpush/).

Recovery has two halves. Configuration: a zone export captures DNS records only, so WAF, Access, and TLS settings need cf-terraforming or at least documented screenshots stored privately. Access: Cloudflare's account recovery without a device or backup codes depends on a previously verified browser and takes three to five days that cannot be expedited, which is why the control asks for two separately accountable super administrators with tested backup codes. Rehearse the compromise response too: change the password, revoke sessions, roll every key and token, and read the audit log. [Import and export DNS records](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/manage-dns-records/how-to/import-and-export/), [Import Cloudflare resources to Terraform](https://developers.cloudflare.com/terraform/advanced-topics/import-cloudflare-resources/), [Account recovery](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/user-profiles/account-recovery/), [Secure a compromised account](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/account/account-security/secure-a-compromised-account/).

## Risk scenarios

The [risk assessment template](risk-assessment.csv) holds twelve generic scenarios for this platform, from a registrar takeover to an unnoticed certificate failure. Each row names the threat source, the affected assets, and the controls in `related_controls` that would reduce it, using the library's [1 to 3 likelihood and impact scale](../../documentation/cyber-risk/risk-assessment-and-treatment.md) with the same bands: 1 to 2 Low, 3 to 4 Moderate, 6 High, 9 Critical. The inherent rating assumes the related controls are absent; the residual rating assumes they operate as asserted, which is exactly what the assessment output tells you. Every status is `Proposed`, owners are roles, and dates, evidence, and acceptance authority are placeholders.

The two files are meant to be read together. A control that comes back `gap` or `unknown` means the residual rating of every scenario that lists it is not yet earned and should be treated as inherent until evidence exists. A `not_applicable` control with a current scope reason removes the scenario only if the workload really is absent; a missing licence does not. Copy the register outside the repository, replace the scenarios with the firm's own wording, and record real owners, dates, and acceptance decisions in the private copy.

## The first review session

Run the [fictional example](observed.example.json) with the [local assessment tool](../README.md) using `--as-of 2026-09-20`; it produces seven aligned assertions, three gaps, nine unknowns, and one scoped not-applicable control, including a stale observation, a missing evidence reference, an unsupported implementation, and an approved exception that still shows as a gap. For a real review, copy the worksheet and the risk register outside the repo and choose five priority questions: who can unlock the domain, which tokens can change DNS, whether the origin answers directly, which leaver still matches an Access policy, and who would notice a certificate or security alert. Record uncertainty. Then use the [staged rollout plan](../rollout-and-exceptions.md) to turn evidence into specific changes.
