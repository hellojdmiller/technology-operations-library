# AWS: a baseline for technology operations

I would start with the blast radius. A small firm that runs a handful of workloads on AWS usually has one or two people who can reach everything, a few long-lived credentials nobody remembers creating, and backups that have never been restored. The account structure, the identity path, and the log trail decide whether a single mistake stays small. A useful baseline protects those three things first and lets the workloads themselves stay simple.

This guide is a proposed operating standard, not a certification. [Twenty control assertions](desired-state.json), the [evidence worksheet](evidence-worksheet.csv), and a [risk assessment template](risk-assessment.csv) make it reviewable. They are not settings to import, a CloudFormation template, or a Security Hub configuration policy. The linked AWS documentation was reviewed on **2026-09-20**; each control carries its own source and review gate.

## Make the scope explicit

Before rating anything, record the organization structure (management account, organizational units, member accounts), the enabled Regions and the home Region, the external identity provider and its provisioning method, the workloads and data classes per account, the recovery objectives, and which paid services are actually enabled. Keep the real details outside this repository.

Several controls depend on services that cost money or must be switched on per account and per Region: Amazon GuardDuty, AWS Security Hub CSPM, AWS Config, IAM Access Analyzer unused-access analyzers, AWS Backup restore testing, AWS Secrets Manager, and customer managed KMS keys. The pages I reviewed state no additional charge for AWS Organizations, AWS Backup Vault Lock, and the first copy of CloudTrail management events, and mention no separate charge for S3 Block Public Access, EBS encryption by default, or IMDSv2 defaults; storage, KMS requests, Config items, and Lambda rotation still bill. Recording "not enabled" is a budget decision to make visible, not a reason to mark a risk as absent.

The [local assessment tool](../README.md) compares reviewer assertions recorded in a private copy of the worksheet. It does not sign in to an account, call an AWS API, read a finding, or open an evidence reference. An `aligned` result means a reviewer said the assertion held for the whole declared scope with fresh evidence; it is not an independent check of the account.

Use three example populations when designing a pilot: a platform administrator, a developer or operator who owns one workload, and a finance or operations user who only reads reports. Add one third-party integration that authenticates programmatically. The purpose is to expose a business dependency before a guardrail does.

## Account structure and guardrails: controls 01 to 02

The management account is the one place service control policies cannot reach, so I would keep it empty: no workloads, no data, and access limited to named administrators whose access is reviewed on a schedule. Delegate security services to a separate account where the service supports it. [Management account best practices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_best-practices_mgmt-acct.html) and the [Security Reference Architecture view of the management account](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/security-reference-architecture/management-account.html).

Service control policies set the maximum available permissions for member accounts; they grant nothing, they require all features enabled, and they do not restrict service-linked roles. AWS recommends against attaching an untested SCP at the root; move a pilot OU first. Denying `organizations:LeaveOrganization` and `account:CloseAccount` at the root is now an AWS default for organizations created through the console after July 10, 2026, and a manual step for everyone else. [SCP behavior](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_manage_policies_scps.html) and [SCP examples](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_manage_policies_scps_examples.html).

The acceptance test is practical: a member-account administrator cannot stop the organization trail, disable GuardDuty, or leave the organization, and the pilot record shows which business action was tried and denied.

## Identity, credentials, and emergency access: controls 03 to 07

Root users need MFA in every account (AWS now requires registration for root users of every account type, with a 35-day grace period after first sign-in), no access keys, a group mailbox, and a reviewed recovery phone number held by different people from the mailbox. For member accounts, AWS supports centrally removing root credentials so nobody can sign in as root or recover the password without going through the management account. [Root user best practices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/root-user-best-practices.html) and [centralized root access](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/id_root-enable-root-access.html). Rehearse the privileged-task flow before deleting credentials.

Human access should federate through IAM Identity Center from the firm's external identity provider with SCIM provisioning. One detail matters for evidence: IAM Identity Center's own MFA settings are not supported when an external identity provider is the identity source, so the MFA policy must be enforced and evidenced at the provider. [External identity providers](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/singlesignon/latest/userguide/manage-your-identity-source-idp.html) and [MFA scope](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/singlesignon/latest/userguide/enable-mfa.html).

Long-lived IAM user credentials for people should not exist. The [IAM credential report](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/id_credentials_getting-report.html) lists passwords, the first two access keys per user, and MFA status; treat any human-owned key as a gap with an expiry, and any workload key as a candidate for an IAM role. [IAM security best practices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/best-practices.html) describe the remaining legitimate long-term cases, such as third-party tools that cannot use Identity Center.

Permission sets should map to job functions, with administrative sets assigned to named operators only. [Permission sets](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/singlesignon/latest/userguide/permissionsetsconcept.html) become IAM roles in each account; [IAM Access Analyzer](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/what-is-access-analyzer.html) unused-access findings support the quarterly review but are billed per role and user analyzed.

Emergency access must be built and tested before an outage. AWS documents a direct-federation pattern for an IAM Identity Center disruption that still depends on the external provider, and the Well-Architected guidance describes dedicated break-glass users with MFA, no access keys, and alerts on use for the case where federation itself is down. [Emergency access setup](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/singlesignon/latest/userguide/emergency-access.html) and [SEC10-BP05](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/security-pillar/sec_incident_response_pre_provision_access.html). Store only the exercise result and an evidence reference in an assessment, never the credentials.

## Logging and detection: controls 08 to 11

Create one multi-Region organization trail that delivers to a bucket in a dedicated log account, enable log file validation, and restrict who can read the bucket. Member accounts can see the trail but cannot change it. The trail covers enabled Regions only; opt-in Regions and other partitions need their own decision. [Organization trails](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/awscloudtrail/latest/userguide/creating-trail-organization.html), [log file integrity validation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/awscloudtrail/latest/userguide/cloudtrail-log-file-validation-intro.html), and the [Log Archive account pattern](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/security-reference-architecture/log-archive.html).

Alarms need the trail delivered to CloudWatch Logs first. AWS documents metric filters for security group changes, console sign-in failures, and IAM policy changes; add root sign-in and changes to the trail or to GuardDuty. An alarm that emails an unmonitored mailbox is a gap, not a control. [CloudWatch alarms for CloudTrail](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/awscloudtrail/latest/userguide/cloudwatch-alarms-for-cloudtrail.html).

GuardDuty is Regional. Designate one delegated administrator (the same account in every Region), turn on auto-enable for new accounts, and enable it in every Region including the ones nobody uses. Then give findings an owner. [GuardDuty with Organizations](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/guardduty/latest/ug/guardduty_organizations.html) and [Regions](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/guardduty/latest/ug/guardduty_regions.html).

AWS now names the posture product Security Hub CSPM and offers a separate Security Hub; record which one is enabled. The [AWS Foundational Security Best Practices standard](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/securityhub/latest/userguide/fsbp-standard.html) runs mostly on AWS Config rules, so [Config recording must exist in each Region](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/securityhub/latest/userguide/securityhub-setup-prereqs.html) for accurate findings, and [central configuration](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/securityhub/latest/userguide/securityhub-accounts.html) through a delegated administrator avoids Region-by-Region drift. [AWS Config](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/config/latest/developerguide/WhatIsConfig.html) itself bills per configuration item and rule evaluation.

## Data protection: controls 12 to 15

Turn on all four S3 Block Public Access settings at the account level in every account, or enforce them with an organization policy. The setting rejects new public policies and ignores public ACLs but does not rewrite existing policies, so removing it re-exposes anything that was public before. [Block Public Access](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/access-control-block-public-access.html) and [account-level configuration](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/configuring-block-public-access-account.html).

S3 encrypts new objects with SSE-S3 by default; the baseline asks for SSE-KMS with a firm-owned key where the data is confidential, and for [EBS encryption by default](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ebs/latest/userguide/encryption-by-default.html) in each used Region. Neither setting encrypts existing objects or volumes. [S3 default encryption](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/default-bucket-encryption.html).

Every KMS key has exactly one key policy, and key policies are Regional. Separate key administrators from key users, enable automatic rotation, and deny deletion; a key that disappears makes the backups and logs it protected unreadable. [Key policies](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/kms/latest/developerguide/key-policies.html) and [key rotation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/kms/latest/developerguide/rotate-keys.html).

Application and database credentials belong in [Secrets Manager](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/secretsmanager/latest/userguide/intro.html) with access limited to the consuming roles; [rotation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/secretsmanager/latest/userguide/rotating-secrets.html) is managed for supported services and otherwise runs through a Lambda function you pay for. AWS credentials themselves belong in IAM roles, not in secrets.

## Network exposure: controls 16 to 17

Security groups are allow-only and aggregate across every group attached to a resource, so a review has to read the effective set per Region. No administrative or database port should accept traffic from anywhere; database instances should have public access turned off and sit in private subnets. [Security group rules](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/vpc/latest/userguide/security-group-rules.html) and [RDS public accessibility](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/USER_VPC.WorkingWithRDSInstanceinaVPC.html).

IMDSv2 defends instance role credentials against request forgery. Set the account-level default to require IMDSv2 in each Region and enable enforcement so that IMDSv1 launches fail; existing instances are not changed by the default and must be modified individually, and containers may need a hop limit of 2. [Instance metadata options](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/configuring-instance-metadata-options.html) and [account defaults](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/configuring-IMDS-new-instances.html). If the firm runs no EC2 instances, record that as a scoped not-applicable with inventory evidence and a trigger for reassessment.

## Backup, cost, and change control: controls 18 to 20

Assign every in-scope data store to an [AWS Backup plan](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-backup/latest/devguide/about-backup-plans.html), then protect the recovery points from the administrator who could delete them: copy them to a [separate account](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-backup/latest/devguide/create-cross-account-backup.html) (requires AWS Organizations and a non-default destination vault) or hold them in a vault with [Vault Lock](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-backup/latest/devguide/vault-lock.html). Compliance-mode locks cannot be removed after the grace period, so agree retention before locking. Then prove a restore with [restore testing](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-backup/latest/devguide/restore-testing.html) or a manual exercise; a successful backup job settles nothing about recovery.

Budgets and anomaly detection are cheap abuse signals. [AWS Budgets](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cost-management/latest/userguide/budgets-managing-costs.html) updates up to three times a day and can alert on forecasted spend; [Cost Anomaly Detection](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cost-management/latest/userguide/manage-ad.html) can take a day to detect and needs history to learn. Treat an anomaly alert as a security triage item, not only a finance one.

Define production in version-controlled templates and review changes before execution. [CloudFormation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSCloudFormation/latest/UserGuide/Welcome.html) [change sets](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSCloudFormation/latest/UserGuide/using-cfn-updating-stacks-changesets.html) preview the effect but do not guarantee success. Require owner and environment [tags](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/tag-editor/latest/userguide/tagging.html) and standardize them with a [tag policy](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_manage_policies_tag-policies.html); tag policies evaluate only the tags they define, so an untagged resource is a separate check.

## Risk scenarios

The [risk assessment template](risk-assessment.csv) lists twelve generic scenarios for this platform: a leaked long-lived access key, a public bucket or shared snapshot holding diligence material, a root user without MFA, deleted backups with no independent copy, a GuardDuty finding nobody owns, an identity provider outage with no emergency path, an over-privileged permission set, a public database endpoint, credential theft through IMDSv1, a stopped or altered trail, runaway spend after credential compromise, and a secret committed to a repository. No row describes a real organization, account, or incident.

Each row's `related_controls` field names the control ids from [desired-state.json](desired-state.json) that would reduce it. Likelihood and impact use the library's 1 to 3 scale and bands from the [risk assessment and treatment method](../../documentation/cyber-risk/risk-assessment-and-treatment.md): 1 to 2 Low, 3 to 4 Moderate, 6 High, 9 Critical. The inherent rating describes the scenario with none of the related controls in place. The residual rating assumes every related control operates and is evidenced, which in assessment terms means each related control shows `aligned`. If any related control is a gap or unknown, the residual rating has not been earned and the inherent rating stands until evidence arrives.

Every row is `Proposed`, with `Unassigned` and `Pending` placeholders for dates, verification evidence, and acceptance authority. The columns extend the [library risk register template](../../library/templates/risk-register.csv) with threat source, affected assets, related controls, and written ratings so the register and the baseline can be read together. Replace the scenarios, agree the scoring with the sponsor, and keep the completed register in the firm's protected record.

## The first review session

Run the [fictional example](observed.example.json) with the [local assessment tool](../README.md):

```sh
node baselines/scripts/assess.mjs baselines/aws/desired-state.json baselines/aws/observed.example.json --as-of 2026-09-20
```

It reports 7 aligned, 3 gaps, 9 unknown, and 1 not applicable, and includes a stale observation, a missing evidence reference, an unsupported service, an approved exception that still shows as a gap, and a scoped not-applicable control. Every value in it is invented.

For a real review, copy the worksheet and the risk template outside the repository and choose five priority questions: who can reach the root users and the management account, whether a leaked key would be noticed before the invoice, whether every account and Region is logged and watched, whether a public bucket or database could exist without someone knowing, and whether a backup can be restored without depending on a key or an administrator who might be gone. Record uncertainty as unknown. Then use the [staged rollout plan](../rollout-and-exceptions.md) to turn evidence into specific, reversible changes.
