# First 30 days of cyber-risk review

Build a defensible picture of the firm's exposure and the next decisions. The schedule is a planning example, not a promise that a firm can be assessed or secured in 30 days. Active incidents and exposed critical services take precedence.

For each row record an owner, scope, observation date, private evidence reference, result, and next action. Use **supported, gap, unknown, or not applicable with rationale** for the checklist result. A checked box means the review was performed; it does not mean the control passed.

## Days 1–5: establish the boundary

- [ ] Agree the management-company, fund, vendor, and portfolio boundaries. Output: approved scope and escalation contacts. Escalate any service being operated without clear authority.
- [ ] Identify the business activities that cannot wait, including reporting, capital activity, deal access, and critical communications. Output: business owners and agreed impact scenarios; leave recovery objectives unassigned until approved.
- [ ] Reconcile a service list from identity applications, purchasing, device management, and owner interviews. Output: known services plus unresolved coverage. A single discovery source is not a complete inventory.
- [ ] Identify privileged people, service identities, integrations, external support, and emergency access. Output: who can change what and the tested way to recover administration.
- [ ] Confirm incident escalation and independent communications. Output: a reachable response owner and alternate; do not conduct an unannounced production incident simulation.

## Days 6–10: inspect controls where failure matters

- [ ] Review effective authentication and access policies for representative identities, including exclusions. Output: configuration evidence plus allowed/denied tests in an authorized scope.
- [ ] Reconcile active staff, contractors, devices, and critical SaaS access. Output: unmatched records and ownership questions; a mismatch is a review item, not automatic permission to disable an account.
- [ ] Inspect device support, encryption, management coverage, and urgent exposure. Output: coverage denominators and unknown devices. Agree remediation priority with the service owner before scheduling disruptive changes.
- [ ] Walk through a fictional payment-change request with Finance. Output: proof that an independently established verification route and separate authorization are understood.
- [ ] Identify restricted repositories and external sharing. Output: a representative permission review, its sampling limit, and unresolved inheritance or guest access.

## Days 11–20: prove visibility and recovery

- [ ] Follow a safe synthetic security event from collection to triage. Output: event time, ingestion time, alert/routing result, and response ownership; a dashboard license is insufficient.
- [ ] Review retention and retrieval for required evidence sources. Output: what can be retrieved for what interval, by whom, including edition and configuration constraints.
- [ ] Restore an approved synthetic sample to an isolated destination. Output: content, freshness, permissions, elapsed time, and business acceptance. Backups do not resolve every provider outage.
- [ ] Review critical vendor dependencies, support access, and exit methods. Output: current evidence, unresolved contract questions, and substitute operating paths.
- [ ] Inventory approved AI use cases and connectors. Output: owner, data boundary, actions, model/tool configuration, and restrictions; use the [AI review](../ai/ai-use-case-review.md).

## Days 21–30: make the next decisions concrete

- [ ] Consolidate overlapping findings into business scenarios. Output: prioritized risks with evidence confidence, accountable owners, and decision dates.
- [ ] Propose a treatment backlog sized to actual staff capacity. Output: first actions, dependencies, acceptance tests, and the cost of delaying them.
- [ ] Record formal exceptions in the protected system. Output: authorized decision, expiry, review trigger, and compensating evidence; missing approval remains an unresolved gap.
- [ ] Run one [tabletop](../scenarios/tabletop-exercises.md). Output: decision gaps and assigned improvements, not a declaration of operational recovery.
- [ ] Present the [leadership brief](../reporting/leadership-risk-brief.md). Output: decisions requested, material uncertainty, and next review.

## Stop and escalate

If evidence suggests ongoing unauthorized access, data loss, or fraud, use the incident process. If the review lacks authority, scope, or a recovery route for a proposed test, keep that test pending. Preserve originals and evidence access restrictions. Do not close an unknown by replacing it with a favorable assumption.
